Diversity in the Living World

Read top to bottom — it follows the order of the chapter. Tick each section off as you finish it.

1 · Biodiversity and the nature walk

  • Guides: Dr Raghu (scientist) and Maniram chacha (mimics bird calls); teacher Madam Sulekha.
  • Each bird has a unique chirp → diversity in nature.
  • Biodiversity = variety of plants and animals in a particular region. Plants and animals depend on each other (shelter, food, seed spreading).
  • Rule of the walk: observe without disturbing; don't pluck leaves or flowers.

5 · Adaptations — one line each

  • Cactus: fleshy stem stores water (hot desert).
  • Deodar: conical shape + sloping, flexible branches → snow slides off (Himalayas).
  • Rhododendron: short with small leaves in windy Nilgiris; taller in Sikkim.
  • Hot-desert camel: long legs, wide hooves (no sinking), one hump.
  • Cold-desert camel: shorter legs (mountains), two humps (shrink in late winter), long hair head-to-neck.
  • All camels: little urine, dry dung, no sweat → many days without water.

6 · Habitats

  • Habitat = where a living thing lives; gives food, water, air, shelter.
  • Terrestrial: forests, deserts, grasslands, mountains. Aquatic: ponds, lakes, rivers, oceans.
  • Amphibians (frogs) live in both.

8 · 10 facts most likely to be asked

  1. Biodiversity = variety of plants and animals in a region.
  2. Tree / shrub / herb — mango / rose / tomato.
  3. Reticulate venation — hibiscus; parallel — banana, grass.
  4. Taproot — mustard; fibrous — grass.
  5. Dicot = 2 cotyledons (chickpea); monocot = 1 (maize).
  6. Dicot → reticulate + taproot; monocot → parallel + fibrous.
  7. Adaptation = special feature to survive in a region.
  8. Habitat = where a plant/animal lives; terrestrial vs aquatic; frogs are amphibians.
  9. Cold-desert camel: two humps, shorter legs, long hair. Hot-desert: one hump, long legs, wide hooves.
  10. Project Tiger 1973; Cheetah project 2022; Salim Ali = Birdman of India.

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't lose marks here
  • Calling a shrub a "small tree" — shrubs have many stems branching near the ground; trees have one thick trunk with branches high up.
  • Mixing up the pairs: reticulate goes with taproot, parallel goes with fibrous — never the other way round.
  • Saying dicots have "two seeds" — they have two cotyledons in one seed.
  • Saying camels store water in their humps — the chapter says they store food.
  • Giving the cold-desert camel one hump — it has two; the hot-desert camel has one.
  • Confusing adaptation (a feature) with habitat (a place).
  • Writing Salim Ali as the "Tiger Man" or Janaki Ammal as a bird expert — Ali = birds; Ammal = plants (botanist).
