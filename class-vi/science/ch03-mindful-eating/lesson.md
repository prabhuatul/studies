# Science — Chapter 3: Mindful Eating: A Path to a Healthy Body

> **Class VI | CBSE / NCERT — Curiosity: Textbook of Science, Grade 6**

## Overview

This chapter explores what we eat, why we eat it, and how food keeps our body healthy. It covers the major components of food — carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals, roughage, and water — along with simple tests to identify these nutrients. The chapter also introduces the concept of a balanced diet, the importance of millets, the dangers of junk food, and the idea of food miles (how far food travels from farm to plate).

## Core Concepts

### 3.1 What Do We Eat?

People across India eat a diverse variety of food. The type of food consumed in a region depends on:
- The crops cultivated locally
- Taste preferences
- Culture and traditions

**Regional variety (Activity 3.2):** Different states grow different crops and have unique food traditions. For example, Punjab is known for wheat-based foods, Karnataka for rice-based dishes, and Manipur for its own local varieties.

#### How Have Cooking Practices Changed Over Time?

Cooking practices are also called **culinary practices**. Over the years, these have changed significantly:

| Traditional | Modern |
|---|---|
| Chulha (earthen stove) | Gas stove / electric stove |
| Sil-batta (stone grinder) | Electric grinder / mixer |

These changes happened because of:
- Technological development
- Improved transportation
- Better communication

---

### 3.2 What Are the Components of Food?

Food contains several important components called **nutrients**. The main nutrients are:

1. Carbohydrates
2. Fats
3. Proteins
4. Vitamins
5. Minerals

In addition to nutrients, **roughage** and **water** are also essential parts of a healthy diet.

---

#### Carbohydrates

- Carbohydrates are the **primary source of energy** in our diet.
- They are called **energy-giving foods**.
- Glucose (a type of carbohydrate) gives instant energy.
- Common sugar is also a type of carbohydrate.

**Sources of carbohydrates:**
- Cereals: wheat, rice, maize
- Vegetables: potato, sweet potato
- Fruits: banana, pineapple, mango

---

#### Fats

- Fats are a source of **stored energy** in the body.
- They are also called **energy-giving foods**.
- Fats provide more energy than carbohydrates.

**Sources of fats:**

*From plants:*
- Nuts: groundnuts, walnuts, almonds
- Seeds: pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds
- Coconut
- Oils: sunflower oil, soybean oil, coconut oil

*From animals:*
- Curd, butter, ghee

**Fun Fact:** Polar bears accumulate a thick layer of fat under their skin as an energy reserve during hibernation (their long winter sleep).

---

#### Proteins

- Proteins are important for the **growth and repair** of the body.
- They are called **body-building foods**.
- Sportspersons need larger quantities of proteins to build and repair muscles.

**Sources of proteins:**

*From plants (Fig. 3.4a):*
- Pulses: moong dal, tuar dal
- Beans: kidney beans, gram, soya bean
- Peas
- Nuts

*From animals (Fig. 3.4b):*
- Milk, paneer, egg, fish, meat, chicken

**Note:** Mushrooms are also a good source of protein.

---

#### Vitamins and Minerals — Protective Nutrients

- Required only in **small amounts** but are essential.
- They protect the body from diseases.
- **Vitamin D** is special — it is naturally produced by the body when skin is exposed to **sunlight**.
- **Iodine** (a mineral) can be obtained from seawater or rocks.

**Important tip about cooking:**
- Some nutrients (especially Vitamin C) are lost during cooking because of high heat.
- Washing vegetables after cutting or peeling them also causes loss of vitamins.
- **Always wash vegetables before cutting or peeling them.**

---

#### Vitamins and Minerals — Full Reference Table (Fig. 3.5)

| Nutrient | Function | Sources | Deficiency Disease | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin A | Keeps eyes and skin healthy | Papaya, carrot, mango, milk | Loss of vision / Night blindness | Poor vision, loss of vision in darkness, sometimes complete loss of vision |
| Vitamin B₁ | Keeps heart healthy; supports the body to perform various functions | Legumes, nuts, whole grains, seeds, milk products | Beriberi | Swelling, tingling or burning in feet and hands, trouble breathing |
| Vitamin C | Helps the body fight diseases | Amla, guava, green chilli, orange, lemon | Scurvy | Bleeding gums, slow healing of wounds |
| Vitamin D | Helps the body absorb calcium for bone and teeth health | Sunlight exposure, milk, butter, fish, eggs | Rickets | Soft and bent bones |
| Calcium | Keeps bones and teeth healthy | Milk / soya milk, curd, cheese, paneer | Bone and tooth decay | Weak bones, tooth decay |
| Iodine | Helps perform physical and mental activities | Seaweed, water chestnut (singhada), iodised salt | Goitre | Swelling at the front of the neck |
| Iron | Important component of blood | Green leafy vegetables, beetroot, pomegranate | Anaemia | Weakness, shortness of breath |

#### Roughage (Dietary Fibre)

- Roughage does **not** provide nutrients to the body.
- It is still essential because it:
  - Helps the body get rid of undigested food
  - Ensures smooth passage of stools (prevents constipation)
- Roughage comes mainly from **plant products**.

**Sources of roughage:**
- Green leafy vegetables
- Fresh fruits
- Whole grains
- Pulses
- Nuts

---

#### Water

- Water is an essential part of our diet.
- It helps the body **absorb nutrients** from food.
- It **removes waste** from the body through sweat and urine.
- We should drink sufficient water regularly to stay healthy.

### 3.3 How to Test Different Components of Food?

#### Test for Starch (Activity 3.5)

**Materials:** Potato, cucumber, bread, boiled rice, boiled gram, crushed peanuts, oil, butter, crushed coconut; dilute iodine solution; dropper

**Method:**
1. Place a small amount of each food item on a separate dish.
2. Add 2–3 drops of dilute iodine solution using a dropper.

**Result:** If the food item turns **blue-black**, starch is present.

---

#### Test for Fats (Activity 3.6)

**Method:**
1. Place the food item on a separate piece of paper.
2. Wrap the paper around the food and press it.
3. If the food contains water, allow the paper to dry.
4. Hold the paper against a source of light.

**Result:** If an **oily / greasy patch** appears on the paper (light shines through it), fat is present.

---

#### Test for Proteins (Activity 3.7 — Teacher Demonstration)

**Materials:** Pestle and mortar, test tube, copper sulphate solution, caustic soda solution

**Method:**
1. Make a paste or powder of the food item using a pestle and mortar.
2. Put the paste in a test tube.
3. Add 2–3 teaspoons of water.
4. Add 2 drops of copper sulphate solution.
5. Add 10 drops of caustic soda solution.
6. Shake gently and leave undisturbed.

**Result:** If the mixture turns **violet**, protein is present.

**Note:** Peanuts show the presence of both proteins and fats. Most foods contain more than one nutrient.

**PRECAUTIONS:**
- Copper sulphate and caustic soda are harmful chemicals.
- Handle with care.
- If spilled on skin or clothes, wash immediately with plenty of water.
- Do not taste or smell these chemicals.

---

|---|---|---|---|
| Potato | Present | Absent | Absent |
| Cucumber | Absent | Absent | Absent |
| Boiled rice | Present | Absent | Absent |

### 3.4 Balanced Diet

Nutritional requirements are not the same for everyone. They vary according to:
- Age
- Gender
- Physical activity level
- Health status
- Lifestyle

**Definition:** A **balanced diet** is a diet that has all essential nutrients, roughage, and water in the **right amount** for proper growth and development of the body.

A balanced diet ensures that the body gets enough energy-giving foods (carbohydrates and fats), body-building foods (proteins), and protective foods (vitamins and minerals).

---

### 3.5 Millets: Nutrition-Rich Cereals

**Millets** are small-grained cereals that are native crops of India. They include:
- Jowar (Sorghum)
- Bajra (Pearl millet)
- Ragi (Finger millet)
- Sanwa (Barnyard millet)

**Why are millets important?**
- Can be grown easily in different climatic conditions (drought-resistant)
- Have been an integral part of the Indian diet for centuries
- Are excellent sources of vitamins, minerals (especially iron and calcium), and dietary fibre
- Are also called **nutri-cereals** because of their high nutritional value
- Contribute significantly to a balanced diet

---

#### Packaged Food and FSSAI

- Packaged food must display **nutritional information** on the cover, showing the amount of each nutrient per serving or per 100 g.
- **Fortification** means adding nutrients to food during processing to improve its nutritional quality. Examples: iodised salt, certain fortified baby foods.
- **FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India)** is the government agency that ensures food quality and safety in India.

---

#### Junk Food

- Junk food is high in calories because it contains large amounts of sugar and fat.
- It has very low amounts of proteins, minerals, vitamins, and dietary fibre.
- Examples: potato wafers, candy bars, carbonated drinks (fizzy drinks)
- Consuming junk food frequently can lead to **obesity** and several other health problems.

**Nutritional Comparison — Potato Wafers vs Roasted Chana (Activity 3.9, per 100 g):**

| Nutrient | Potato Wafers | Roasted Chana |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | 536 kcal | 355 kcal |
| Fats | 35.0 g | 6.26 g |
| Carbohydrates | 53.0 g | 58.58 g |
| Proteins | 7.0 g | 18.64 g |
| Dietary Fibre | 4.8 g | 16.8 g |

**Observation:** Roasted chana has far less fat, more protein, and nearly 3.5 times more dietary fibre than potato wafers — making it a much healthier snack choice.

### 3.6 Food Miles: From Farm to Our Plate

**Food miles** refer to the entire distance a food item travels from the producer (farmer) to the consumer (us).

**Why should we try to reduce food miles?**
- Cuts down the cost and pollution caused during transportation
- Supports local farmers and their livelihoods
- Keeps food fresher and more nutritious

**Journey of a Chapati — From Farm to Plate (Fig. 3.10):**

```
Farmer growing wheat
        ↓
Threshing and winnowing of grains
        ↓
Storage of grains
        ↓
Grinding of grains and packing (into flour)
        ↓
Transport to retail shop
        ↓
Food on our plate
```

**Message from the chapter:**
> "Eat healthy, share, and respect food. Support local producers!"

Eating locally grown and plant-based food is healthy for our body and also good for the environment and our planet.

## Important Rules / Formulas

- **Starch Test:** 2–3 drops of dilute iodine solution → blue-black colour = starch present
- **Fat Test:** Press food on paper, allow to dry, hold against light → oily/transparent patch = fat present
- **Protein Test:** Copper sulphate + caustic soda solution → violet colour = protein present
- **Balanced Diet Rule:** All nutrients (carbohydrates + fats + proteins + vitamins + minerals) + roughage + water in correct amounts
- **Vitamin C Precaution:** Wash vegetables before cutting or peeling to prevent vitamin loss
- **Iodised Salt:** Common salt + iodine salts — prevents goitre

## Case Studies — Deficiency Diseases in History

### Case Study 1: Scurvy

- **Who discovered it?** Scottish physician **James Lind** in **1746**
- **Where?** Sailors on long sea voyages
- **Symptoms:** Bleeding and swollen gums
- **Discovery:** Sailors who ate lemons and oranges recovered; those who did not continued to suffer
- **Conclusion:** Scurvy is caused by **Vitamin C deficiency**; citrus fruits (lemons, oranges) cure it

### Case Study 2: Goitre

- **When?** 1960s
- **Who?** Indian scientists
- **Where?** Himalayan region and Northern plains of India
- **Observation:** Swelling at the front of the neck was common in these areas
- **Cause:** Iodine deficiency — the soil in these regions lacked iodine, so crops grown there also lacked iodine
- **Solution:** The Government of India supplemented common salt with iodine → **iodised salt**
- **Note on salt production:** The **Agariyas**, a tribal community in the **Little Rann of Kutch, Gujarat**, have a traditional practice of extracting salt from seawater. They live in extreme desert heat for about 8 months of the year doing this work.

## Know a Scientist: Coluthur Gopalan (1918–2019)

- Pioneer of nutrition research in India
- Analysed more than 500 Indian foods for their nutritional value
- Recommended an appropriate diet suited to the Indian context
- Led large surveys on the nutritional status of the Indian population
- Identified widespread deficiencies in protein, energy, and other food components across India
- His work directly led to the implementation of the **Mid Day Meal Programme** in 2002, now called the **'PM POSHAN'** initiative — which provides balanced meals in government-run and government-aided schools across India

## Quick Summary

- People across India eat diverse types of food; choices depend on local crops, culture, taste, and traditions.
- Cooking practices (culinary practices) have changed from traditional methods (chulha, sil-batta) to modern methods (gas stove, electric grinder) due to technology and better connectivity.
- The main food components (nutrients) are: carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals. Roughage and water are also essential.
- Carbohydrates and fats are energy-giving foods; proteins are body-building foods; vitamins and minerals are protective foods.
- Each vitamin and mineral has specific functions; their deficiency causes specific diseases (e.g., Vitamin C deficiency → Scurvy; Iodine deficiency → Goitre; Vitamin D deficiency → Rickets; Iron deficiency → Anaemia).
- Starch can be detected using iodine solution (blue-black colour); fats using paper test (oily patch); proteins using copper sulphate and caustic soda (violet colour).
- A balanced diet contains all nutrients, roughage, and water in the right proportions.
- Millets (jowar, bajra, ragi, sanwa) are nutrient-rich native Indian cereals, also called nutri-cereals.
- Junk food is high in sugar and fat but low in proteins, vitamins, minerals, and fibre — frequent consumption leads to obesity and health problems.
- Food miles measure the distance food travels from farm to plate; eating locally grown food is healthier and better for the environment.
- Coluthur Gopalan's nutrition research led to the PM POSHAN (Mid Day Meal) programme in India.
- Always wash vegetables before cutting or peeling to preserve vitamins.
