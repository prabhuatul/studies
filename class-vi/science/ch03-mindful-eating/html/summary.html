1 · Food and culinary practices

  • Food choice depends on local crops, taste, culture and traditions.
  • Chulha → gas stove; sil-batta → mixer — due to technology, transport, communication.

4 · Food tests

  • Starch: iodine → blue-black.
  • Fat: paper → oily, translucent patch.
  • Protein: copper sulphate + caustic soda → violet.
Watch outSugar is a carbohydrate but not starch, so it does not turn blue-black with iodine.

5 · Balanced diet, millets, junk food

  • Balanced diet = all nutrients + roughage + water, right amounts. Needs vary with age, gender, activity, health.
  • Millets: jowar, bajra, ragi, sanwa — nutri-cereals.
  • Junk food: high sugar & fat, low protein/vitamins/fibre → obesity.
  • Fortification (iodised salt); FSSAI regulates food safety.

6 · Food miles and history

  • Food miles = distance from farmer to consumer; eat local.
  • James Lind, 1746 — lemons/oranges cured scurvy in sailors.
  • 1960s goitre in Himalayan region & northern plains → iodised salt.
  • C. Gopalan (1918–2019) — 500+ Indian foods analysed → Mid Day Meal (2002) → PM POSHAN.

7 · 10 facts most likely to be asked

  1. Carbohydrates are the primary source of energy.
  2. Proteins are body-building foods.
  3. Vitamins and minerals are protective foods.
  4. Starch + iodine → blue-black.
  5. Protein test → violet colour.
  6. Vitamin C deficiency → scurvy; Vitamin D → rickets.
  7. Iodine deficiency → goitre; iron → anaemia.
  8. Vitamin D is made in the body in sunlight.
  9. Millets (jowar, bajra, ragi, sanwa) are nutri-cereals.
  10. Food miles = distance from farm to plate.

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't lose marks here
  • Saying roughage gives nutrients — it gives none, but it is still essential.
  • Mixing up the test colours — blue-black = starch, violet = protein.
  • Saying sugar turns blue-black with iodine — only starch does.
  • Writing Vitamin C → rickets — it is scurvy; rickets is Vitamin D.
  • Washing vegetables after cutting — wash before, to keep vitamins.
  • Thinking a balanced diet means "eat equal amounts of everything" — it means right amounts for your needs.
