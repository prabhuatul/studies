### Section D: Short Answer Questions — Answers

**1. What is a balanced diet? Why is it important for a Class VI student?**

A balanced diet is a diet that contains all essential nutrients (carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals), along with roughage and water, in the right amounts. It is important for a Class VI student because the body is growing rapidly at this age and needs nutrients for energy (to study and play), body building (to grow taller and stronger), and protection from diseases (to stay healthy and attend school regularly).

**2. What is the role of roughage in our diet? Name any three sources of roughage.**

Roughage (dietary fibre) helps the body get rid of undigested food. It ensures the smooth passage of stools, preventing constipation and keeping the digestive system healthy. It does not provide nutrients directly, but it is still essential for good health. Three sources of roughage are: green leafy vegetables, fresh fruits, and whole grains (such as whole wheat bread or brown rice).

**3. Why should we eat locally grown food? Give two reasons related to health and environment.**

Eating locally grown food keeps it fresher and more nutritious, because it does not travel long distances and so loses fewer vitamins and minerals. From an environmental point of view, buying local food reduces the distance food travels (food miles), which cuts down on pollution and carbon emissions from vehicles used in transportation. It also supports local farmers.

**4. What is iodised salt, and why did the Government of India make it widely available?**

Iodised salt is common salt that has been mixed with small, required quantities of iodine salts. In the 1960s, Indian scientists found that people living in the Himalayan region and northern plains of India suffered from goitre (swelling at the front of the neck) because the soil and water in those areas lacked iodine. To address this deficiency on a large scale, the Government of India ensured that common salt (which everyone uses daily) was fortified with iodine, making iodised salt widely available.

**5. State the precautions that must be taken while performing the protein test in the laboratory.**

The chemicals used in the protein test — copper sulphate and caustic soda (sodium hydroxide) — are harmful. The following precautions must be taken: (a) Handle the chemicals carefully and avoid direct contact with skin or eyes. (b) If the chemicals spill on skin or clothes, wash the area immediately with plenty of water. (c) Do not taste or smell these chemicals under any circumstances. (d) This test should be done only as a teacher demonstration for safety.

### Section E: Long Answer Questions — Answers

**1. Main food components and their importance:**

Our food contains several important components called nutrients, plus roughage and water.

- **Carbohydrates** — These are the primary energy-giving foods. They provide the body with the energy needed for all activities. Sources: rice, wheat, banana, potato.

- **Fats** — These also provide energy and store it in the body for later use. Fat stored under the skin even helps keep the body warm. Sources: butter, ghee, groundnuts, coconut oil.

- **Proteins** — These are body-building foods. They are needed for the growth of the body and for repairing damaged cells and muscles. Sportspersons need extra proteins to build muscles. Sources: dal (pulses), eggs, milk, paneer, chicken.

- **Vitamins** — These are protective nutrients needed in small amounts. Different vitamins do different jobs — Vitamin A keeps eyes healthy, Vitamin C helps the body fight diseases, and Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium for strong bones. Sources vary: carrots and papaya for Vitamin A; amla and oranges for Vitamin C; sunlight and eggs for Vitamin D.

- **Minerals** — These are also protective nutrients. Calcium keeps bones and teeth strong; iron is an important part of blood; iodine supports physical and mental activities. Sources: milk for calcium; green leafy vegetables and beetroot for iron; iodised salt for iodine.

- **Roughage** — Does not give nutrients but keeps the digestive system working properly by helping remove undigested food. Sources: whole grains, fruits, vegetables.

- **Water** — Helps absorb nutrients and removes waste from the body through sweat and urine.

**2. Food miles and the journey of a chapati:**

**Food miles** refers to the total distance a food item travels from the producer (the farmer) to the consumer (us, who eat it). Every step in the supply chain adds to the food miles.

The journey of a chapati from farm to our plate goes through these steps:
1. A farmer grows wheat in the field.
2. After harvest, the wheat is threshed (grain separated from stalk) and winnowed (husk separated from grain).
3. The clean grain is stored in a warehouse or granary.
4. The stored grain is taken to a mill where it is ground into flour and packed.
5. The flour is transported by trucks or other vehicles to retail shops.
6. We buy the flour, make dough, and cook chapatis at home.

At each step, fuel is burned and pollution is created. If we buy wheat flour from a local mill or farmer nearby, the food miles are much less, meaning less fuel is used and less pollution is produced.

Eating locally grown food is better because: (a) it is fresher and retains more nutrients, (b) it reduces pollution from transportation, (c) it supports local farmers economically, and (d) it is better for the planet. The chapter's message is: "Eat healthy, share, and respect food. Support local producers!"

### Section F: HOTS — Answers

**1. Riya's family diet analysis:**

Riya's family diet of rice, dal, and pickles provides some carbohydrates (rice) and some protein (dal), but it is severely lacking in several nutrients:

- **Missing vitamins and minerals:** There are no vegetables, fruits, or dairy products in the diet. This means they are likely deficient in Vitamin A (needed for eyesight and skin), Vitamin C (needed to fight diseases and heal wounds), calcium (for bones and teeth), and iron (for blood health — iron deficiency causes anaemia, leading to tiredness and weakness, which the brother experiences).

- **Missing roughage:** Without fruits and vegetables, there is very little dietary fibre, which affects digestion.

**Suggestions to make the diet more balanced:**
- Add green leafy vegetables like spinach (palak) or methi — these provide iron, calcium, and Vitamin A.
- Add seasonal fruits like guava, banana, or papaya — these give Vitamin C and Vitamin A.
- Include curd or milk — for calcium and protein.
- Include eggs or a wider variety of pulses (rajma, chana) — for more complete protein.
- Add a millet like bajra or ragi rotis instead of plain rice sometimes — for iron, calcium, and fibre.

This would turn a very limited diet into a balanced one without spending much money, since all these foods are locally available in India.

**2. The sailor with scurvy:**

The sailor's symptoms — bleeding gums and wounds that heal very slowly — are the classic signs of **scurvy**, which is caused by a deficiency of **Vitamin C**.

Looking at the foods available on the ship:
- Dried biscuits, salted meat, sugar, and dried peas — these have little or no Vitamin C (vitamins, especially Vitamin C, are destroyed by long storage, drying, and heat).
- **Lemons** — these are a rich source of Vitamin C.

The sailor should eat the lemons every day. Vitamin C in the lemons will help his body fight the disease, repair damaged blood vessels (stopping the gum bleeding), and speed up wound healing.

This connects directly to the discovery by Scottish physician **James Lind** in 1746, who observed that sailors who ate citrus fruits (lemons and oranges) on long voyages recovered from scurvy, while others who did not eat them remained sick. Modern nutritional science confirms that Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is essential for the formation of collagen — the protein that holds skin, blood vessels, and tissues together. Without enough Vitamin C, these break down, causing the symptoms of scurvy. Today, we know that eating fresh fruits (especially citrus fruits, amla, and guava) every day easily prevents scurvy.
