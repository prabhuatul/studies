# Science — Chapter 5: Measurement of Length and Motion

> **Class VI | CBSE / NCERT — Curiosity: Textbook of Science, Grade 6**
> Textbook pages 79–100
> Last updated: 2026-10-10

---

## Overview

Deepa, an eleven-year-old from Haryana, needs a new uniform — and wonders why the tailor uses a tape, the shopkeeper a rod, and her mother says _char angula_. With her friends Anish, Hardeep, Padma and Tasneem, she discovers that **body-part units** (handspan, arm, stride) give different answers for different people, which is why the world adopted **standard SI units** — the **metre**. The chapter teaches the **correct way to measure** (placing the scale, eye position, broken scales, curved lines), how to **write units**, and then moves to **position**: the **reference point**, what **motion and rest** really mean, and the types of motion — **linear, circular, oscillatory** and **periodic**.

---

## Opening Thought

> **Deepa's puzzle:** The shopkeeper measured two metres of cloth with a **metal rod**; the tailor used a **flexible tape**; and her mother asked to lengthen the uniform by _char angula_ (four fingers' width). Are these like the scale in a geometry box? What is _char angula_?

## Key Terms & Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Measurement | Comparing an unknown quantity with a known fixed quantity (a unit). |
| Unit | The fixed quantity used for comparison — e.g. handspan, metre. A length is written as a number and a unit. |
| Handspan (balisht) | The distance across a spread hand — a body-part unit that differs from person to person. |
| Angula | Finger width — an ancient Indian unit; still used by carpenters and tailors. _Char angula_ = four fingers' width. |
| Dhanusa, yojana | Larger ancient Indian units of length mentioned in ancient literature. |
| SI units | The International System of Units — standard units adopted by countries. |
| Metre (m) | The SI unit of length. 1 m = 100 cm. |
| Centimetre (cm) | One-hundredth of a metre. 1 cm = 10 mm. |
| Millimetre (mm) | One-tenth of a centimetre (1 mm = 0.1 cm) — the smallest length a 15-cm scale can measure. |
| Kilometre (km) | 1000 metres — used for large distances. |
| Inch | An older unit still on some scales; 1 inch = 2.54 cm. |
| Reference point | A fixed object or point with respect to which distance or position is stated. |
| Motion | An object is in motion if its position changes with respect to the reference point with time. |
| Rest | An object is at rest if its position does not change with respect to the reference point with time. |
| Linear motion | Motion along a straight line. |
| Circular motion | Motion along a circular path. |
| Oscillatory motion | Motion to and fro about a fixed position. |
| Periodic motion | Motion that repeats its path after a fixed interval of time — circular and oscillatory motion are periodic. |

## Core Concepts

### 5.1 How do we measure? — body-part units

Deepa's friends recall people measuring with body parts: Hardeep's grandmother measures cloth by the **length of her arm**; farmers count **strides** to divide fields into beds; some use the **length of their feet**. Deepa's mother uses the **handspan**, which she calls **_balisht_**.

The five friends measure the same table with their handspans:

| Student | Number of handspans |

| Anish | Slightly more than 13 |

| Padma | 13 |

| Tasneem | Slightly less than 13 |

| Deepa | Between 13 and 14 |

| Hardeep | 14 |

The numbers differ because **their handspans are of different sizes**. The handspan here is a **unit**, and a length is expressed in **two parts — a number and a unit** (13 handspans: 13 is the number, handspan the unit).

> **The problem:** Body-part units differ from person to person. We need a unit for which measurements of the same length by different people **do not differ**.

### India's ancient measurement systems

India has a rich history of measurement. **Angula** (finger width), multiples of angula, **dhanusa** and **yojana** are units mentioned in ancient Indian literature, used for measuring **artefacts, architecture and town planning**. The angula is still used by traditional craftspeople such as **carpenters and tailors**. Objects with ruled markings that could be scales have been excavated from sites of the **Harappan Civilisation**.

### 5.2 Standard units — SI and the metre

Different systems of units grew up in different places. When people travelled, this caused **confusion**, so countries came together and adopted standard units: the **International System of Units (SI units)**.

- The **SI unit of length is the metre**, symbol **m**.

- 1 m is divided into **100 equal divisions**, each a **centimetre (cm)**.

- On a **15-cm scale**, each centimetre is divided into **10 equal parts**, each a **millimetre (mm)**. 1 mm is the **smallest length** you can measure with it; 1 mm = 0.1 cm.

- For large lengths we use the **kilometre (km) = 1000 m**.

  1 km = 1000 m      1 m = 100 cm      1 cm = 10 mm

> **Do you know?:** Some scales also show **inches**: 1 inch = **2.54 cm**. Inch and foot were used earlier and are still used by some people.

### 5.3 The correct way of measuring length

- **Choose an appropriate scale** — 15-cm scale for a pencil; metre scale or measuring tape for a room's height; a **flexible tape** (tailor's tape) for the girth of a tree or your chest.

- **Place the scale in contact with the object along its length** (Fig. 5.4).

- **Keep your eye directly above** the point being read — e.g. directly above the tip of the pencil (position B in Fig. 5.5). Looking from the side gives a wrong reading.

- **Broken scale / unclear zero:** start from any other full mark, say 1.0 cm, and **subtract** that reading from the reading at the other end.

> **Do you know?:** Visually challenged students use scales with **raised markings** that can be felt by touch.

### Writing units correctly

- Unit names (kilometre, metre, centimetre, millimetre) begin with a **lowercase** letter, except at the start of a sentence.

- Symbols **km, m, cm, mm** are lowercase and are **never followed by 's'** for the plural.

- **No full stop** after the symbol, except at the end of a sentence.

- Always leave a **space between the number and the unit** — "15 cm", not "15cm".

Activity 5.1: measure a comb, pen, pencil and eraser with a metre scale, always writing the number **and** the unit, and compare with friends' measurements.

### 5.4 Measuring the length of a curved line

Anish's family put string lights along the arches of their verandah — how much would they need? For a **curved line**, use a **flexible measuring tape**, or lay a **thread** along the curve, mark it, then **straighten the thread** and measure it with a metre scale (Fig. 5.8).

### 5.5 Describing position — the reference point

Going to a garden for a class visit, the friends disagree about whether the garden or the school is closer: Tasneem and Padma say the garden, Deepa and Anish say the school, Hardeep says about equal. **All of them are correct** — each is measuring from a different house. If they had all measured from **one point, such as the bus stand**, their answers would agree.

**When distance is stated with respect to a fixed object or point, that point is called a reference point.**

- **Kabaddi court:** before drawing lines with _chuna_ (limestone powder), Deepa says they must first fix a **reference point** to measure from.

- **Kilometre stones:** on the way to Delhi, Padma reads "Delhi 70 km", then "Delhi 60 km". **Delhi is the reference point**; her position is 70 km, then 60 km from it — it is **changing with time**.

### 5.6 Moving things — motion and rest

**An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to the reference point with time. If it does not change its position with respect to the reference point with time, it is at rest.**

**Deepa on the bus:** passengers stay in their seats — compared with herself or the bus, they are **at rest**. Compared with a building outside, they (and the bus) are **in motion**.

> **The big idea:** Whether something is at rest or in motion **depends on the reference point** you choose.

> **Think it over:** On a ship moving at constant speed in a straight line on a calm sea, with no windows — can you tell if it is moving? (Hard to tell: with nothing outside to use as a reference point, everything inside seems at rest.)

### 5.7 Types of motion

| Type | Definition | Examples from the chapter |

| **Linear** | Moves along a **straight line** | A dropped eraser, an orange falling from a tree, the Republic Day march-past, a heavy box being pushed, a car on a straight road |

| **Circular** | Moves along a **circular path** | An eraser whirled on a thread, a merry-go-round |

| **Oscillatory** | Moves **to and fro about a fixed position** | A hanging eraser pulled aside and released, a swing, the free end of a metal strip pressed to a table |

> **More to know — periodic motion:** If an object **repeats its path after a fixed interval of time**, its motion is **periodic**. Circular motion goes round the same path again and again, and oscillatory motion repeats its to-and-fro — so **both are periodic**.

## Important Rules / Formulas

- **1 km = 1000 m · 1 m = 100 cm · 1 cm = 10 mm · 1 mm = 0.1 cm**.
- 1 inch = 2.54 cm.
- Length = **number + unit**.
- Broken scale: **length = reading at far end − reading at starting mark**.
- Scale in contact along the length; eye directly above the reading point.
- Motion = position changes w.r.t. a reference point **with time**.
- Linear = straight line; circular = circular path; oscillatory = to and fro about a fixed position; circular and oscillatory are periodic.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Measuring with a broken scale (Fig. 5.6)

An object is placed with one end at the **1.0 cm** mark; the other end is at **10.4 cm**.

**Solution:**

- Length = reading at far end − reading at starting end
- = 10.4 cm − 1.0 cm
- = **9.4 cm**

### Example 2: Converting kilometres to metres (Exercise Q5)

The distance between school and home is **1.5 km**. Express it in metres.

**Solution:**

- 1 km = 1000 m
- 1.5 km = 1.5 × 1000 m
- = **1500 m**

### Example 3: One height, three units (Exercise Q7 style)

A friend's height is **1.42 m**. Express it in cm and mm.

**Solution:**

- 1 m = 100 cm → 1.42 m = 1.42 × 100 = **142 cm**
- 1 cm = 10 mm → 142 cm = 142 × 10 = **1420 mm**

### Example 4: Measuring distance with a bicycle wheel (Learning further)

A wheel's outer boundary measures **2 m** (using a string). The wheel turns **50** times.

**Solution:** distance = boundary × number of turns = 2 m × 50 = **100 m**. A device called a **Jones Counter** uses this idea to measure road-race distances.

## Activities

### Activity 5.1 — Let us measure

Measure a comb, pen, pencil and eraser with a metre scale; record in Table 5.2 with number and unit; compare with friends and discuss reasons for small differences.

### Activity 5.2 — Let us explore (motion or rest?)

List five objects in motion and five at rest (Table 5.3) with a justification — e.g. a cow grazing in the field (motion), a tree (rest).

### Activity 5.3 — Let us explore (straight-line motion)

Drop an eraser from a height and watch it fall in a straight line — like a falling orange, a march-past, or a pushed box: linear motion.

### Activity 5.4 — Let us investigate (whirling)

Tie an eraser (or potato) to a thread and whirl it — it moves in a circle, like a merry-go-round: circular motion.

### Activity 5.5 — Let us investigate (hanging eraser)

Hang the eraser by the thread, pull it slightly to one side and release — it moves to and fro like a swing: oscillatory motion.

### Activity 5.6 — Let us investigate (metal strip)

Press one end of a thin metal strip (about 50 cm long) to a table with books or a brick; press the free end and release — it moves up and down: oscillatory motion.

### Activity 5.7 — Let us identify (children's park)

In a children's park (Fig. 5.18), classify motions as linear, circular or oscillatory in Table 5.4 — e.g. a swing moving to and fro is oscillatory; a slide is linear; a merry-go-round is circular.

## Quick Summary

- The **International System of Units (SI units)** has been adopted by countries as standard units of measurement.
- The SI unit of length is the **metre** (symbol **m**).
- **1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm.**
- When distance is stated with respect to a fixed object or point, that point is a **reference point**.
- An object is in **motion** if its position changes with respect to a reference point with time.
- **Linear motion**: along a straight line. **Circular motion**: along a circular path. **Oscillatory motion**: to and fro about a fixed position.
- Motion that repeats after a fixed interval is **periodic** — circular and oscillatory motions are periodic.
