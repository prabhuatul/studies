Measurement of Length and Motion

Read top to bottom — it follows the order of the chapter. Tick each section off as you finish it.

1 · Why standard units?

  • Body-part units — handspan (balisht), arm, stride, foot — differ from person to person (Table 5.1: 13 to 14 handspans for the same table).
  • Length = number + unit.
  • Ancient Indian units: angula (finger width), dhanusa, yojana; angula still used by carpenters and tailors. Scales found at Harappan sites.
  • Travel caused confusion → countries adopted SI units. SI unit of length = metre (m).

2 · Conversions

Numbers to memoriseEvery conversion question comes from this line.
Bigger=Smaller
1 km=1000 m
1 m=100 cm
1 cm=10 mm
1 mm=0.1 cm
1 inch=2.54 cm

Big → small: multiply (1.5 km × 1000 = 1500 m). Small → big: divide (250 cm ÷ 100 = 2.5 m).

3 · Measuring correctly — four rules

  1. Right tool: 15-cm scale (pencil) · metre scale/tape (room) · flexible tape (tree girth, chest).
  2. Scale in contact with the object, along its length.
  3. Eye directly above the reading point (position B).
  4. Broken scale: start at a full mark and subtract (10.4 − 1.0 = 9.4 cm).

Curved line → thread along the curve, straighten, measure. Smallest reading on a 15-cm scale = 1 mm.

4 · Writing units

  • Lowercase: metre, km, m, cm, mm.
  • No plural 's': 5 cm, never "5 cms".
  • No full stop after a symbol (except at the end of a sentence).
  • A space between number and unit: 15 cm.

5 · Reference point, motion and rest

  • Reference point = fixed object/point from which distance is stated (bus stand; Delhi on kilometre stones).
  • Motion = position changes w.r.t. reference point with time. Rest = it doesn't.
  • Bus passengers: at rest w.r.t. the bus; in motion w.r.t. a building outside.

6 · Types of motion

TypePathExamples
LinearStraight lineFalling eraser/orange, march-past, pushed box, car on straight road
CircularCircular pathWhirled eraser, merry-go-round, fan blades
OscillatoryTo and fro about a fixed positionSwing, hanging eraser, vibrating metal strip
PeriodicRepeats after a fixed interval — circular and oscillatory motions are periodic.

7 · 10 facts most likely to be asked

  1. SI unit of length: metre (m).
  2. 1 km = 1000 m; 1 m = 100 cm; 1 cm = 10 mm.
  3. Handspan is not a standard unit.
  4. Eye directly above the reading point.
  5. Broken scale: subtract the starting mark.
  6. Curved line: thread or flexible tape.
  7. Reference point = fixed point from which distance is stated.
  8. Motion = change of position w.r.t. reference point with time.
  9. Linear / circular / oscillatory — straight / circle / to and fro.
  10. Circular and oscillatory motion are periodic.

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't lose marks here
  • Writing 1 km = 100 cm — it is 1000 m = 1,00,000 cm.
  • Writing units as "cms", "Cm" or "15cm" — correct is 15 cm.
  • Reading the scale from the side — the eye must be directly above.
  • Using a broken scale from its broken end and forgetting to subtract the starting mark.
  • Saying an object is "simply at rest" without a reference point — rest and motion depend on the reference point.
  • Calling a swing's motion circular — it is oscillatory (to and fro).
  • Forgetting the unit — a length is always number + unit.
