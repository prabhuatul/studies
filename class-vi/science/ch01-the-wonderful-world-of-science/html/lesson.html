The Wonderful World of Science

What science is, why curiosity matters, what this book will explore, and the scientific method you already use every day.

Core Concepts

1 What is Science?

As human beings, we have always been curious about our surroundings, and we start exploring and asking questions right from childhood. In the Middle Stage this journey continues with a new subject — Science.

Science is a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world we live in and to uncover the secrets of the universe. Think of it as a big adventure — we ask questions, explore the world and try to understand how things work.

For this, the most important thing is to have 'Curiosity' — which is where the title of the book comes from.

Examples of everyday mysteriesWhy do the stars shine? How does a flower know when to open? Whether we study tiny grains of sand or massive mountains, a leaf of grass or a vast forest, there is always something new to discover.
2 Science is everywhere

The most wonderful thing about science is that it is everywhere — from the depths of the ocean to the vastness of outer space, from what is cooking in the kitchen to what is happening on the playground.

Some of the most groundbreaking discoveries have often come from unexpected places.

3 Science is like a giant, unending jigsaw puzzle
  • Every new discovery adds another piece to the puzzle.
  • There is no limit to what we can discover — every new piece of knowledge leads to more questions.
  • Sometimes a piece has been put in the wrong place and needs to be moved — new discoveries often change our understanding of the world.
  • As we discover more, we realise that all these ideas are connected.
4 What will we explore with the help of this book?
  • Our home, planet Earth — the only planet we know that supports life; an environment we must protect.
  • The variety of life — plants and animals that survive and thrive in different regions. How does a seed grow into a plant, or a caterpillar become a butterfly?
  • Food — India's many cuisines. What are the dishes made of? How do we find out?
  • Materials — paper, a metal key, a plastic ruler, a rubber eraser, the magnet that keeps the box closed, clothes, a cup. What are they made of? How do we separate materials from one another?
  • Water — why and how does it rain? Water freezes into ice when cooled and boils into steam when heated.
  • Hot and cold — finding out how hot something is (water, or our body when we have fever) can be important.
  • Beyond the Earth — the Sun, the Moon and the millions of stars in the sky.

Whether it is the structure of a leaf, how things move, or separating the skin of a peanut seed, each chapter should ignite your spirit of inquiry.

5 The pen that stopped writing — the scientific method in action
  1. Question: "Why did my pen stop writing?"
  2. Guess: perhaps the ink is finished.
  3. Test: open the pen and check the refill. If it is empty, the guess was correct.
  4. If the guess was wrong (the ink is not finished), make another guess — perhaps the ink has dried up — and try something else to test it.

"This is exactly how Science works!" Finding out why your pen stopped writing is an example of the scientific method.

NoticeA wrong guess is not a failure — it simply leads to the next guess and the next test.
6 The five steps of the scientific method

Science is not just about memorising facts and figures or doing experiments. It is about following a step-by-step process that helps us find answers.

  1. Observe something that we find interesting or do not understand.
  2. This makes us wonder and think of a question about it.
  3. Guess a possible answer to that question.
  4. Test this guess through experiments or more observations.
  5. Analyse the results to see if they actually answered our question.

7 We are all scientists

Scientists are people who follow the scientific method to solve problems or to discover new things. But anyone who follows the scientific method is working like a scientist:

  • A cook wondering why the dal spilled out of the cooker — was there too much water?
  • A bicycle repair person finding out why a tyre is flat — from where did the air leak out?
  • An electrician finding why a light bulb is not working — is the problem with the bulb or the switch?

"When we try to ask questions and find out answers, in a way, we are all scientists!" We apply the scientific method in many daily situations, knowingly or unknowingly. Learning science develops our ability to solve bigger problems and more mysteries of the universe.

8 How to learn science well
  • The first and foremost thing is to be curious and observe your surroundings keenly.
  • When we are curious, we start asking how and why. The world is full of things we do not know, waiting to be explored.
  • Science is rarely done alone. Scientists across the world work together, often in large teams — so ask your friends to help. It is more fun to discover things together.
  • You will not find answers to all your questions in Grade 6 — this is a journey for the next five years or even beyond.
  • Much like children enjoying the rain, science is all about joyful exploration.
"After all, to be a wise person, you must be a 'whys' person!"

Important Rules & Facts

  • Science = a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world and uncover the secrets of the universe.
  • The most important thing in science is curiosity.
  • Scientific method, in order: Observe → Question → Guess → Test → Analyse.
  • A guess is tested through experiments or more observations.
  • If a guess is wrong, make a new guess and test again.
  • Anyone who follows the scientific method is working like a scientist.
  • New discoveries can change what we thought we knew (the misplaced jigsaw piece).

Activities in the chapter

1 Activity 1 — Let us think and write (p.5)

Write about a problem similar to the pen example that you tried to solve. What steps did you take?

Model answerMy bicycle light stopped working. I observed it was dark, asked why, guessed the battery was dead, tested by putting in a new battery — it still didn't work, so I made a new guess (a loose wire), tested by fixing the wire, and the light worked. I analysed: the wire was the cause.
2 Activity 2 — Let us think and write (p.6)

Describe a daily-life situation where you feel someone was following a scientific method.

Model answerThe rice in the cooker was not cooking properly. My mother guessed there was too little water, tested by adding a little more water and cooking again, and the rice cooked perfectly — so her guess was right.
3 Activity 3 — Let us think and write (p.7)

If you had to ask "Why?" about something, what would you ask about? Write down how you would try to find the answer.

Model answer"Why does my plant on the window grow bent towards the window?" Guess: it grows towards light. Test: turn the pot around and watch for a week. Analyse: if it bends back towards the window, the guess is supported.
