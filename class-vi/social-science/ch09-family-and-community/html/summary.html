2 · Family relationship terms

  • English has few kinship terms; Hindi has many more — bua, tau, tai, chacha, mausi, nana, nani.
  • Tamil has different terms for elder vs younger siblings.
  • Most Indian languages have no word for 'cousin' — cousins are just 'brothers'/'sisters'.
Remember the tableचाचा (chacha) = father's younger brother. बहन in the example table is used for a female cousin ("mother's brother's daughter"), among other meanings.

5 · Roles and Responsibilities

Family relationships rest on love, care, cooperation (= working together) and interdependence. Parents raise children to be happy, responsible members of society; children take on more responsibility as they grow, learning traditions through daily practice.

6 · Community — rural examples

  • Community = connected families/people, or a group sharing religion/region/work/interest.
  • Communities agree on unwritten 'rules' for shared resources (water, grazing, forest produce) — still true in much of rural India.
Numbers and names to memorise Jhabua (Madhya Pradesh) water crisis → Bhil community's halma tradition → planted trees, dug trenches → objective: serve Mother Earth.
2019: Shri Mahesh Sharma (Shivganga movement) won the Padma Shri for Bhil community work.
Chennai floods, 2015: private/religious groups cooked and distributed food.

7 · Community — urban examples

  • Kamal Parmar (Ahmedabad, Gujarat): auto-fabrication workshop owner; taught underprivileged children 5:30–9:30 pm daily + free dinner; 150 children attended.
  • RWAs (Residents' Welfare Associations): newer (30–40 years), make their own local rules.
  • Communities are interdependent — RWAs depend on traders for supplies, municipal workers for waste.
Flexible concept'Community' can also mean a jāti, or a group by religion/region/work/interest — 'Mumbai's Parsi community', 'Chennai's Sikh community', a school's sports/art community, etc.

8 · Ten facts most likely to be asked

  1. Joint family = several generations; nuclear family = couple + children
  2. No word for 'cousin' in most Indian languages
  3. Four values: ahimsa, dāna, sevā, tyāga
  4. Shalini's story is set in Kerala, at Onam
  5. Tenzing's story is set in Meghalaya
  6. Community rules for shared resources are usually unwritten
  7. Jhabua (MP) + Bhil community + halma tradition
  8. Shri Mahesh Sharma won the Padma Shri in 2019
  9. Kamal Parmar taught 150 children in Ahmedabad
  10. Communities are ultimately interdependent

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't lose marks here
  • Mixing up dāna (giving), sevā (service) and tyāga (sacrifice) — they are three distinct values.
  • Saying Shalini's story is set in Meghalaya, or Tenzing's in Kerala — it's the other way round.
  • Calling the halma tradition "paid work" — it is explicitly unpaid, done as a duty.
  • Thinking community only exists in villages — the chapter gives urban examples too (Kamal Parmar, RWAs).
  • Forgetting that communities are interdependent — no community is fully self-sufficient.
  • Saying a "jāti" is unrelated to "community" — the chapter explicitly calls it one kind of community.
