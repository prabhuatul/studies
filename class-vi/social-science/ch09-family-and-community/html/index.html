What this chapter is about

This chapter looks at the two building blocks of society closest to home: the family — joint or nuclear, the oldest and most fundamental unit of society — and the community, the wider, flexible network of connected families and people. Through real-life stories — Shalini in Kerala, Tenzing in Meghalaya, the Bhil community of Jhabua, and Kamal Parmar's street-side tuition classes in Ahmedabad — it shows how families teach values like ahimsa, giving and sacrifice, how roles and responsibilities are shared, and how communities, rural or urban, are ultimately interdependent.

The six things most worth remembering

1 · Joint vs nuclear

A joint family spans generations under one roof; a nuclear family is a couple (or one parent) and children.

2 · Four family values

Ahimsa, dāna, sevā, tyāga — learned at home, following one's dharma.

3 · Shalini and Tenzing

Kerala's Onam story teaches sharing and sacrifice; Meghalaya's story shows roles adapting when circumstances change.

4 · Community rules, mostly unwritten

Shared resources (water, grazing land) are managed by agreed, rarely-written rules — everyone has a duty to keep things running.

5 · Halma and street classes

The Bhil community's halma tradition (Jhabua) and Kamal Parmar's free classes (Ahmedabad) — rural and urban, same spirit of giving without expecting anything back.

6 · Interdependence

Even a Residents' Welfare Association depends on traders and municipal workers — no community stands alone.
