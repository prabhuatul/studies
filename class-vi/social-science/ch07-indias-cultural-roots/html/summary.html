India's Cultural Roots

Read top to bottom — it follows the order of the chapter. Tick each section off as you finish it.

1 · The Vedas

Veda ← Sanskrit vid, 'knowledge'. Four Vedas: Ṛig, Yajur, Sāma, Atharva — India's oldest texts.

  • Composed as hymns, recited orally, not written — in the Sapta Sindhava region.
  • Ṛig Veda dated anywhere 5th to 2nd millennium BCE by experts.
  • Composed by rishis (male) and rishikas (female) seers.
  • UNESCO, 2008: Vedic chanting = "a masterpiece of the oral and intangible heritage of humanity."
Learn this mantraekam sat viprā bahudhā vadanti — "The Existent is one, sages give it many names."

2 · Vedic society and schools of thought

Society organised into janas (clans, 30+ in the Ṛig Veda). Governance clues: rājā (king), sabha / samiti (assembly).

Key concepts table

TermMeans
YajñaVedic ritual/offering, mainly to Agni (fire)
KarmaOur actions, or their results
RebirthTaking birth again and again
BrahmanThe one divine essence of everything (NOT the god Brahmā)
ĀtmanThe Self — one with brahman
VedantaSchool: everything is one essence, brahman
YogaSchool: realise brahman in one's own consciousness
Don't confuseaham brahmāsmi = "I am brahman" (about the Self) vs tat tvam asi = "You are That" (spoken to someone else, e.g. Śhvetaketu).

5 · Not "religions" — and the Chārvāka exception

The textbook avoids calling Hinduism/Buddhism/Jainism/Sikhism 'religions' — prefers 'schools of thought' / 'belief systems', since they span philosophical, spiritual, ethical and social dimensions.

Chārvāka (Lokāyata): believed only the material world exists, no afterlife. Never popular — disappeared over time. Shows the diversity of belief on offer.

6 · Folk and Tribal Roots

  • Folk traditions = oral, from common people. Tribal traditions = oral, from tribes.
  • Ancient India had no word for 'tribe' — tribes were just janas in forests/mountains. Modern Hindi term: janjāti.
  • Tribe (anthropologists' definition): families/clans, common descent + culture + language, close-knit, under a chief, no private property.
Numbers to memorise2011: 705 tribes, ~104 million people — more than Australia + UK combined.
  • Examples: Khandoba (central India, folk deity → supreme god); Singbonga (Munda & Santhal, eastern India, created the world).
Quote to rememberAndré Béteille: Hinduism and tribal religions have mutually influenced each other throughout history — not a one-way street.

7 · Ten facts most likely to be asked

  1. Four Vedas: Ṛig, Yajur, Sāma, Atharva
  2. UNESCO recognised Vedic chanting in 2008
  3. Upanishads introduced rebirth and karma
  4. Brahman ≠ the god Brahmā
  5. Buddha born in Lumbini; enlightened at Bodh Gaya
  6. Mahāvīra born near Vaishālī, Bihar
  7. 'Jain' ← jina = conqueror (of ignorance)
  8. Ahimsa covers thought, not just action
  9. Chārvāka = no afterlife school, disappeared
  10. 2011: 705 tribes, ~104 million people in India

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't lose marks here
  • Writing "brahman" as the god Brahmā — they are not the same.
  • Saying Jainism is a branch of Buddhism — it is a separate, older-rooted tradition.
  • Saying Buddhism is older than the Vedas — the Vedas came first, by many centuries.
  • Defining ahimsa as only "not hitting someone" — it also covers violent thought.
  • Calling tribal belief systems merely "spirit worship" — they have rich, complex art and cosmologies, and interacted deeply with Hinduism.
  • Mixing up aham brahmāsmi ("I am brahman") with tat tvam asi ("You are That") — same idea, different speaker's viewpoint.
