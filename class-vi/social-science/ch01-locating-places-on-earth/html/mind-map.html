How it all fits together

The chapter answers one question: “how do we say exactly where something is?” It answers it twice — first for a small area (a map), then for the whole planet (the grid). And the grid turns out to give us time as well as place.

How the ideas connect

Five links worth saying aloud — this is what turns facts into understanding.

1 · Round → globe → grid

Earth is round, so we need a globe, so we need a grid. Each step forces the next. The orange-peel test is the proof of the first step.

2 · Twins with different jobs

Latitude and longitude share the same idea — distance from a zero line, in degrees. But latitude gives you climate and longitude gives you time.

3 · Longitude is time

Because the Earth turns a fixed 15° per hour, knowing how far east or west you are is knowing the time. This is the link most students miss.

4 · Truth vs convenience

Local time is the truth; standard time is the convenience. The Gujarat–Assam example is there to show the gap between them.

5 · Two ends of one line

The Prime Meridian and the Date Line are two ends of the same great circle. One is 0° and resets the hour; the other is ~180° and resets the date.

6 · India was there first

The idea of a prime meridian isn’t European. India's madhya rekhā through Ujjayinī predates Greenwich by many centuries.
