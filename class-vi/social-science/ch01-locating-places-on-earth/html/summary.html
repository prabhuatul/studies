2 · Why we need a globe

  • The Earth is nearly a sphere — slightly flattened at the poles.
  • A sphere cannot be shown accurately on flat paper.
  • Orange-peel test: peel into 3–4 big pieces, try to flatten → it tears at the edges.
  • Globe = a sphere with a map drawn on it. Same shape as the Earth, so it represents geography better than a flat map.

6 · India’s own prime meridian

Favourite short-answer topicThree or four marks often hinge on these names — learn the spellings.
  • Greenwich was not the first prime meridian.
  • India’s was the madhya rekhā — ‘middle line’.
  • It passed through Ujjayinī, today Ujjain — a great centre of astronomy, at about 75.8°E.
  • Varāhamihira, a famous astronomer, lived and worked there about 1,500 years ago.
  • Indian astronomers already understood latitude, longitude and the need for a zero meridian. The Ujjayinī meridian was the reference in all Indian astronomical texts.
  • Ancient cities on it: Kurukṣhetra, Rohitaka/Rohtak, Mālavangara/Nagar, Ujjayinī/Ujjain, Māhiṣhmati/Maheshwar, Kumārī/Kanyakumari.

7 · Longitude and time

The Earth spins WEST to EAST · one full turn = 24 hours · one turn = 360°
→ 360 ÷ 24 = 15° PER HOUR (and 1° = 4 minutes)

Rule: go EAST → time is AHEAD (add). Go WEST → time is BEHIND (subtract).

Starting from 12 noon at Greenwich:

45°W30°W15°W0°15°E30°E45°E
9 AM10 AM11 AM12 PM1 PM2 PM3 PM
To find a time differencedifference in longitude ÷ 15 = difference in hours

8 · Local time vs standard time

Local timeStandard time
Based onthe place’s own meridianone meridian chosen for the whole country
Varieswith every meridiansame across the country
The pointa country would need dozens of clocksone convenient clock for all
MemoriseIST = GMT + 5 hours 30 minutes. GMT = Greenwich Mean Time.
→ That is why it is 5:30 pm in India when it is 12 noon in London.

Time zones broadly follow the 15° bands, but the dividing lines are not straight — they bend to follow international borders so a country isn’t split.

On a time-zone map the number inside a country = hours to add to GMT (+) or subtract from GMT (−).

Not one zone per country: USA = 6 zones · Russia = 11 zones (reset your watch 10 times crossing Russia). Half-hour offsets exist too: India +5.5, others +3.5, +4.5, +6.5, +9.5.

9 · International Date Line

  • Sits opposite the Prime Meridian, at approximately 180° longitude.
  • The +12 and −12 time zones touch along it.
Direction of travelDate change
EastwardSubtract a day (Monday → Sunday)
WestwardAdd a day (Sunday → Monday)

Only approximately at 180° — it deviates to avoid splitting a country into two different dates.

10 · Ten facts most likely to be asked

  1. Three components of a map = distance, direction, symbols
  2. Survey of India fixes India’s map symbols
  3. Earth is flattened at the poles
  4. Equator = 0°; poles = 90°N / 90°S
  5. Parallels are unequal, meridians are all equal in length
  6. Prime Meridian fixed at Greenwich in 1884, = 0° longitude
  7. madhya rekhā through Ujjayinī; Varāhamihira, ~1,500 years ago
  8. Earth turns 15° per hour
  9. IST = GMT + 5:30
  10. IDL near 180°; east → subtract a day, west → add a day

Common mistakes to avoid

Don’t lose marks here
  • Saying latitude is 0° at the poles — it is 0° at the Equator
  • Calling NE/SW “cardinal” — they are intermediate directions
  • Thinking latitude affects the time — only longitude does
  • Writing 180°E and 180°W as different meridians — they are the same
  • Saying time-zone lines are exactly the meridians — they bend along borders
  • Reversing the Date Line rule — east = subtract, west = add
