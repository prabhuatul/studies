It answers it twice — first for a small area (**a map**), then for the whole planet (**the grid**). And the grid turns out to give us **time** as well as place.

## Branch 3 · LATITUDE vs LONGITUDE — *the comparison to memorise*

```
              LATITUDE                    |                LONGITUDE
   ---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------
   distance from the EQUATOR              |  distance from the PRIME MERIDIAN
   lines = PARALLELS of latitude          |  lines = MERIDIANS of longitude
   run EAST - WEST                        |  run NORTH - SOUTH (pole to pole)
   FULL circles                           |  HALF circles
   UNEQUAL (Equator longest)              |  ALL EQUAL in length
   0 at Equator -> 90 N / 90 S            |  0 at Greenwich -> 180 E / W
   divides N / S hemispheres              |  divides W / E hemispheres
   controls CLIMATE                       |  controls TIME
```

**The two payoffs — this is the chapter's punchline:**

```
   LATITUDE  ------->  CLIMATE            LONGITUDE  ------->  TIME
        |                                       |
   torrid  (hot, near Equator)             15 degrees = 1 hour
   temperate (moderate, middle)            east = ahead
   frigid  (cold, near poles)              west = behind
```

## Branch 4 · TIME — the chain of reasoning

```
Earth spins WEST to EAST
        |
one full turn = 360 degrees = 24 hours
        |
360 / 24 = 15 DEGREES PER HOUR       <-- the key number
        |
each 15 deg meridian = 1 hour of LOCAL TIME
        |
noon at Greenwich  ->  1pm at 15E,  2pm at 30E,  3pm at 45E
                   ->  11am at 15W, 10am at 30W, 9am at 45W
        |
but a country cannot run on dozens of local times!
        |
so each country adopts ONE STANDARD TIME
        |
INDIA: IST = GMT + 5 hrs 30 min      <-- why 5:30pm here at noon in London
        |
standard times grouped into TIME ZONES
        |
        +--> broadly follow the 15 degree bands
        +--> BUT lines bend to follow INTERNATIONAL BORDERS
        +--> big countries need several: USA = 6, RUSSIA = 11
        |
opposite the Prime Meridian, at about 180 deg:
        |
INTERNATIONAL DATE LINE   (+12 and -12 touch here)
        |
        +--> cross EASTWARD  = SUBTRACT a day  (Mon -> Sun)
        +--> cross WESTWARD  = ADD a day       (Sun -> Mon)
        +--> deviates so no country is split across two dates
```

## How the ideas connect — five links worth saying aloud

1. **Earth is round → so we need a globe → so we need a grid.** Each step forces the next. The orange-peel test is the proof of the first step.
2. **Latitude and longitude are twins with different jobs.** Same idea (distance from a zero line, measured in degrees), but latitude gives you **climate** and longitude gives you **time**.
3. **Longitude *is* time.** Because the Earth turns at a fixed 15° per hour, knowing how far east or west you are *is* knowing what time it is. This is the one link most students miss.
4. **Standard time exists because local time is impractical.** Local time is the truth; standard time is the convenience. The Gujarat–Assam example shows the gap between them.
5. **The Prime Meridian and the Date Line are two ends of the same line.** One is 0°, the other about 180°. One resets the *hour*, the other resets the *date*.
