Core Concepts

1 Introduction — why we need governance

Human beings have been living in communities for a long time. When a large number of people live together, there can be disagreements and disorder, and rules become necessary to maintain order and harmony. Home, school, exams, traffic, and the workplace all have rules. What would happen if no one followed them? Society would not be able to function.

The process of taking decisions, organising society's life with rules, and ensuring that they are followed, is called governance. The group or system that makes the rules and ensures they are followed is a government. Some of the more important rules are called laws. Rules aren't fixed forever — they can be discussed and revised.

2 The Three Organs of Government

Digital technology has made things easier — but also created cybercrime, a new class of criminals stealing money digitally. Fighting it shows how the three organs of government work together:

  • Legislature — makes new laws (or 'legislates'); sometimes updates/removes existing ones. Done by an assembly of representatives.
  • Executive — implements ('executes') the laws. Includes the head of state (president/PM/CM), ministers, and law-enforcement agencies (e.g. the cyber police).
  • Judiciary — the system of courts deciding whether the law was broken, and what punishment follows. Also examines whether executive decisions or laws themselves are fair.

Cybercriminals who are arrested and convicted are usually fined as well as jailed.

The three organs must be kept separate, although they interact and work together — the 'separation of powers' (Fig. 10.3). This provides checks and balances: each organ can check what another is doing and restore balance if one acts beyond its role.

3 Three Levels of Government

India's government functions at three levels or tiers — local, State, and national (Central) (Fig. 10.4). Comparison: if a bulb won't light, you first check the bulb/switch/fuse; if that fails, call an electrician; if the problem is beyond your home, you go to the Electricity Board — three levels of dealing with a problem.

Flood example: a small local flood is handled locally; a flood across several towns brings in the State Government; a massive flood affecting vast areas brings in the Central Government (relief supplies, the army, etc.).

5 Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam — a "nominal" head who made a real impact

Born in a humble family in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, in 1931, Dr. Kalam was a renowned scientist, nicknamed the 'Missile Man of India' for his role in India's space, missile and nuclear programmes. He served as the 11th President of India, 2002–2007. Despite his high status, he stayed deeply connected to people — especially youth — through his passion for education and innovation. He showed that even a nominal role can have an enormous real impact.

"Look at the sky. We are not alone. The whole universe is friendly to us and conspires only to give the best to those who dream and work."

6 Democracy

'Democracy' comes from Greek dēmos ('people') and kratos ('rule'/'power') — literally, 'rule of the people'. But all people can't literally rule at once — just as a whole class can't visit the principal, so a class elects a monitor/representative. The same principle applies at State level (MLAs) and national level (MPs): people vote for representatives who discuss laws and problems in assemblies.

India is a representative democracy — and the world's largest, with about 970 million voters in 2024. In principle, all Indian citizens 18 and above can vote.

Direct democracy example: a class votes by show of hands between two picnic spots — every student's opinion is taken directly.

Grassroots democracy refers to a system enabling ordinary citizens' participation — the base of the government pyramid (Fig. 10.4) — so citizens have a say in decisions that affect them.

Before we move on…
  • No country can run without governance and government.
  • A modern government has three organs — legislative, executive and judiciary — which need to work together.
  • The Indian government functions at three levels — Centre or national, State and local.
  • Democracy is the overall framework for this system. It functions through elected representatives, both at the State and the national levels.

Important Rules & Formulas

  • Governance = decisions + rules + ensuring they're followed. Government = the body that does this. Laws = the more important rules.
  • Three organs: Legislature (makes laws) → Executive (implements laws) → Judiciary (rules on law-breaking, checks fairness).
  • Separation of powers → checks and balances.
  • Three levels: Local → State → Central (Union).
  • National: Supreme Court, Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha, President (nominal) + PM (executive head). State: High Court, State Assembly/Vidhan Sabha, Governor (nominal) + CM (executive head).
  • Democracy = dēmos + kratos = "rule of the people". India: representative democracy, ~970 million voters (2024), voting age 18+.

Quick Summary

  • Governance = decisions + rules + ensuring compliance; government = the body doing this; laws = the more important rules.
  • Three organs: Legislature (makes laws), Executive (implements/enforces), Judiciary (rules on breaches, checks fairness).
  • Separation of powers keeps the organs distinct but interacting — providing checks and balances.
  • Three levels of government: Local → State → Central (Fig. 10.4 pyramid).
  • National: Supreme Court, Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha), President (nominal) + PM (executive head). State: High Court, State Assembly, Governor (nominal) + CM (executive head).
  • Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, 'Missile Man of India', 11th President (2002–2007) — proof a nominal head can have real impact.
  • Democracy = dēmos + kratos = "rule of the people." India: representative democracy, world's largest (~970 million voters, 2024; voting age 18+).
  • Direct democracy (e.g. a class picnic vote) vs grassroots democracy (ordinary citizens' participation at the base level).
  • Mottos: Satyameva Jayate (Govt of India) and Yato Dharmastato Jayah (Supreme Court).
