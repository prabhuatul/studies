Grassroots Democracy – Part 1: Governance

Read top to bottom — it follows the order of the chapter. Tick each section off as you finish it.

1 · Governance basics

  • Governance = taking decisions + organising society with rules + ensuring they're followed.
  • Government = the group/system that makes rules and ensures compliance.
  • Laws = the more important rules. Without rules, "society would not be able to function."
Opening quote"The ruler protects dharma and dharma protects those who protect it." — The Mahābhārata

2 · Three organs — remember L-E-J

OrganRole
LegislatureMakes (and updates/removes) laws
ExecutiveImplements the laws — head of state, ministers, agencies (e.g. cyber police)
JudiciaryRules on law-breaking; checks whether laws/executive decisions are fair
Watch outSeparation of powers keeps the three organs separate but interacting — giving checks and balances.

3 · Three levels of government

The most examined comparison in the chapterLearn this table by heart.

Education is shared by both levels.

Electrician analogyBulb/switch/fuse (local) → electrician (State) → Electricity Board (Central) — three levels of dealing with a problem, just like local/State/Central government.

5 · Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

  • Born Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, 1931. Nicknamed the 'Missile Man of India'.
  • 11th President of India, 2002–2007.
  • Proved that even a nominal role can have a huge real impact.
Remember the acronymsF.A.I.L. = "First Attempt In Learning" · E.N.D. = "Effort Never Dies" · N.O. = "Next Opportunity"

6 · Democracy

  • Democracy = Greek dēmos (people) + kratos (rule) = "rule of the people".
  • India = representative democracy — MLAs (State) and MPs (national) represent citizens.
  • Direct democracy = everyone's opinion taken directly (class picnic vote).
  • Grassroots democracy = ordinary citizens' participation at the base of the pyramid.
Numbers to memorise~970 million voters in India (2024) · voting age 18+

7 · Ten facts most likely to be asked

  1. Three organs: Legislature, Executive, Judiciary
  2. Separation of powers → checks and balances
  3. Three levels: Local, State, Central
  4. National judiciary = Supreme Court; State judiciary = High Court
  5. National legislature = Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; State = Vidhan Sabha
  6. President (nominal, national) vs Governor (nominal, State)
  7. Dr. Kalam = 11th President, 2002–2007, 'Missile Man of India'
  8. Democracy = "rule of the people" (dēmos + kratos)
  9. India: representative democracy, ~970 million voters (2024)
  10. Voting age in India: 18+

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't lose marks here
  • Mixing up the President (national, nominal) with the Governor (State, nominal) — or either with the actual executive head (PM/CM).
  • Saying the President or Governor has full executive power — they are nominal heads.
  • Confusing the legislature (makes laws) with the executive (enforces laws) — they are different organs.
  • Thinking "direct democracy" and "representative democracy" are the same — India uses the latter.
  • Forgetting that Education is a shared function of both Central and State governments.
  • Getting the mottos backwards — Satyameva Jayate is India's national motto; Yato Dharmastato Jayah is the Supreme Court's.
