## Overview

This chapter looks at all the different kinds of "work" people do around us — some paid, some not — and sorts them into **economic activities** (which involve money or money's worth) and **non-economic activities** (done out of love, care, gratitude or duty, without payment). Through the stories of Anu and Kabir's neighbourhood and Kavya's family, it introduces **value addition**, the different ways people are compensated (salary, fee, wage, payment in kind), and why non-economic activities like **sevā** and community participation (Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, Van Mahotsav) matter just as much to society as paid work.

---

## Key Terms & Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Economic activities** | Activities that involve money or are performed in exchange for money or money's worth. E.g. a business person selling school bags, a farmer selling produce, a lawyer earning a fee, a truck driver, factory workers. |
| **Non-economic activities** | Activities that do not generate income or wealth but are done out of feelings like gratitude, love, care and respect. E.g. parents cooking for the family, helping children with schoolwork, youth caring for grandparents, family members helping renovate the house. |
| **Market** | A place where people engage in the exchange of goods and services. People may exchange goods/services for other goods, but in most markets these are exchanged for money. |
| **Fee** | A payment made to a person or organisation in exchange for professional advice or services — e.g. a fee paid to a doctor or a lawyer. |
| **Money's worth** | The monetary value that a person places on an object, based on the benefit they derive from it. |
| **Salary** | A fixed, regular payment generally paid monthly by an employer to an employee. |
| **Wage** | A payment made by the employer to the worker for a specific period of time. |
| **Payment in kind** | A non-cash payment received by a worker for work performed (e.g. goods of equal value instead of cash). |
| **Value addition** | The increase in monetary value created at each stage of transforming something into another form — e.g. turning wood into a finished chair. |
| **Sevā** | Selfless service — seen at temples, gurudwaras, mosques and churches (e.g. langars/community kitchens serving free food), done out of gratitude and to contribute to society without expecting anything in return. |

## Core Concepts

### 1. Opening story — Anu, Kabir and their neighbourhood

Anu and Kabir were playing in the park when they saw their neighbour, **Geeta Aunty**, getting out of a taxi, returning home from work in her uniform. She is a **pilot in the Indian Air Force**, the pride of the whole town. Anu notices Aunty's uniform looks like one she saw at Kabir's house — it belongs to Kabir's **grandfather**, who retired from the **Border Security Force** and now spends his time:
- providing free Geography classes to the neighbourhood kids,
- tending to the vegetable garden, and
- running errands for the home.

Kabir's parents run a shop selling uniforms and ready-made garments — Appa leaves early, Maa joins him after the children leave for school. Maa is usually back home by the time the children return, and she also contributes to a **voluntary group that teaches knitting** to women in the community. Kabir's elder brother **Rohan** is a software engineer at a company that makes computer applications; on weekends he **volunteers** to teach computer skills to those in a nearby college's youth development programme.

Kabir: "Wow, Anu, everyone around us does so many things every day!"

**Opening quotation (Swami Vivekananda):** *"When you are doing any work, do not think of anything beyond. Do it as worship, as the highest worship, and devote your whole life to it for the time being."*

### 2. Economic activities

Different activities are divided into two groups: **economic activities** and **non-economic activities**.

**Economic activities** are those that involve money or are performed in exchange for money or **money's worth** for the parties involved. Examples: a business person selling school bags in the **market**, a farmer selling produce in the market, a lawyer arguing a case and earning a **fee**, a truck driver transporting goods, workers employed in a car manufacturing factory.

### 3. Non-economic activities

**Non-economic activities** are those that do not generate income or wealth but are done out of feelings like gratitude, love, care and respect. Examples: parents cooking food for the family or helping children with schoolwork, youth taking care of grandparents, family members helping renovate the house.

**LET'S EXPLORE** — In the table from Anu/Kabir's story, label the third column 'economic/non-economic activity' and classify each. Geeta Aunty, the Air Force pilot, receives a **salary** and performs an economic activity. Anu's brother Rohan works for a software company and is given a salary (economic activity); over the weekend, he volunteers teaching computer skills (a **non-economic** activity).

**THINK ABOUT IT** — When Kabir's grandfather voluntarily teaches the neighbourhood kids for free, is that economic or non-economic? How is it different from your teachers teaching you at school? What non-economic activities are important to you and your family? Why are they valuable?

### 4. Types of Economic Activities — Kavya's family

Kavya was excited to visit her aunt's house in the village of **Kamlapur**. On the way, she saw a highway being built near the village — a construction site abuzz with activity, large machines (excavators, bulldozers) operated by dedicated technicians. The new highway promised to reduce travel time to the nearest town from **five hours to two hours**. Kavya's **uncle** is a technician in the construction company operating a bulldozer, and receives a **monthly salary** for his services.

The next day, Kavya's aunt finished her household chores and left for work — she is employed at the **village post office**, paid a monthly salary. In the evening, after office work, she also conducts **online classes** to help students prepare for school exams, charging a **weekly fee**.

On the weekend, Kavya visited a local mango orchard with her aunt. There she saw **Sahil**, a farm labourer using a tractor to till a farmer's land. He earns a **daily wage**: part in cash, and the remaining part in mangoes of equal value — this non-cash portion is called **payment in kind**.

**THINK ABOUT IT** — On your way from home to school today, can you recall the various economic activities people are engaged in? In what ways do you think those people are paid?

As we now understand, economic activities are those that have value in terms of money. Economic activities also add value at each stage of the process of transforming something into another form — this is called **value addition**.

### 5. Value addition — Rajesh the carpenter

Kavya's father, **Rajesh**, is a carpenter:
1. He buys wood from a nearby market for **₹600** to make furniture.
2. He uses special tools and materials to build the furniture.
3. He sells a chair for **₹1,000** in the market.

The wood costs ₹600, so what is the remaining ₹400 (₹1,000 − ₹600) for? It is the **monetary value of Rajesh's skill, time and effort** that went into making the chair. Rajesh has added value to the wood by turning it into furniture. From buying the wood to selling the chair, these activities involve payment — they are therefore part of **economic activities**.

### 6. The Importance of Non-Economic Activities

While non-economic activities may not involve money, the value they generate is important in our lives.

**Sevā: selfless service.** We see sevā in many places — temples, gurudwaras, mosques and churches. For instance, **langars** or community kitchens at gurudwaras serve food to every visitor for free. These practices foster a sense of satisfaction and gratitude for what we have, and are a way of contributing to society without expecting anything in return.

### 7. The strength of community participation

The **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** is based on the collective efforts of all Indian citizens to keep surroundings clean. Individually, people keep their homes clean; people have also come together to clean up streets, roads, parks and other public or community areas — together leading to a clean home, neighbourhood, society and nation.

Another example of collective community participation is the celebration of **Van Mahotsav** ('festival of forests') in India, to promote awareness about the value of trees and the conservation of forests. The initiative brings together members of the community for tree plantation drives.

**THINK ABOUT IT** — Many communities in India have similar practices involving community participation — can you identify a few from your area? During festivals, people gather to organise activities, decorate together, and share food they cook — are these non-economic activities? Why do you think they still hold value? Can you identify community programmes undertaken by your school or locality? What did you observe?

- We also learnt about the value addition that economic activities create.
- We understood how non-economic activities contribute to social welfare, personal wellbeing and enhance the overall quality of life.

## Important Rules / Formulas

- **Economic activity** = involves money/money's worth. **Non-economic activity** = done out of love/care/gratitude, no payment.
- **Value addition = Selling price − Cost of raw material** (e.g. Rajesh's chair: ₹1,000 − ₹600 = ₹400 value added through skill, time and effort).
- **Ways people are compensated**: **salary** (fixed, regular, usually monthly), **fee** (for professional advice/services), **wage** (payment for a specific period of work), **payment in kind** (non-cash payment, e.g. goods of equal value).
- A single person can do **both** economic and non-economic activities (e.g. Rohan: salaried software job = economic; weekend volunteering = non-economic).
- Non-economic activities still create real value: social welfare, personal wellbeing, and quality of life.

## Quick Summary

- **Economic activities** involve money or money's worth (e.g. selling goods, earning a fee/wage/salary). **Non-economic activities** involve no payment, done out of love, care, gratitude or duty.
- **Market** = where goods/services are exchanged (usually for money). **Fee** = payment for professional services. **Salary** = fixed regular (usually monthly) pay. **Wage** = pay for a specific period of work. **Payment in kind** = non-cash payment.
- **Value addition**: the extra monetary value created by skill, time and effort — e.g. Rajesh turning ₹600 of wood into a ₹1,000 chair adds ₹400 of value.
- The same person can do both — e.g. Rohan's salaried software job (economic) and weekend volunteering (non-economic); Geeta Aunty's paid Air Force service is still economic despite serving the country.
- **Sevā** (selfless service) — langars, temple prasād distribution — contributes to society without expecting anything in return.
- **Community participation**: **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** (cleanliness) and **Van Mahotsav** ('festival of forests', tree plantation) show the strength of collective, non-economic community effort.
- Non-economic activities contribute to **social welfare, personal wellbeing, and quality of life** — just as real a contribution as economic activities, even without a price tag.
