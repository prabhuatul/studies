What this chapter is about

This chapter looks at all the different kinds of "work" people do — some paid, some not — and sorts them into economic activities (involving money or money's worth) and non-economic activities (done out of love, care, gratitude or duty). Through the stories of Anu and Kabir's neighbourhood and Kavya's family, it introduces value addition, the different ways people are compensated (salary, fee, wage, payment in kind), and why non-economic activities like sevā and community participation (Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, Van Mahotsav) matter just as much to society as paid work.

The six things most worth remembering

1 · One test sorts everything

Did money change hands? Yes → economic. No, but done out of care → non-economic.

2 · Four ways to get paid

Salary (regular), fee (professional services), wage (a period of work), payment in kind (non-cash).

3 · Rajesh's chair, in numbers

₹600 wood → ₹1,000 chair → ₹400 of value addition, from skill, time and effort.

4 · One person, both roles

The same person can have a paid job and volunteer — neither one cancels the other out.

5 · Sevā = value without a price tag

Langars and temple prasād contribute to society without expecting anything in return.

6 · Community effort scales up

Swachh Bharat Abhiyan and Van Mahotsav turn small individual acts into nationwide impact.
