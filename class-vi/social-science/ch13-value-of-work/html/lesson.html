The Value of Work

Economic vs non-economic activities, how people get paid, value addition, and why non-economic work like sevā and community participation matters just as much.

Key Terms & Definitions

10 Every term you need to know
TermDefinition
Economic activitiesActivities that involve money or are performed in exchange for money or money's worth.
Non-economic activitiesActivities that do not generate income or wealth but are done out of feelings like gratitude, love, care and respect.
MarketA place where people engage in the exchange of goods and services.
FeeA payment to a person/organisation for professional advice or services (e.g. a doctor, a lawyer).
Money's worthThe monetary value a person places on an object, based on the benefit they derive from it.
SalaryA fixed, regular payment generally paid monthly by an employer to an employee.
WageA payment made by the employer to the worker for a specific period of time.
Payment in kindA non-cash payment received by a worker for work performed.
Value additionThe increase in monetary value created at each stage of transforming something into another form.
SevāSelfless service — e.g. langars/community kitchens serving free food, done without expecting anything in return.

Core Concepts

1 Opening story — Anu, Kabir and their neighbourhood

Anu and Kabir see their neighbour Geeta Aunty, a pilot in the Indian Air Force, returning home in uniform. Kabir's grandfather (retired from the Border Security Force) provides free Geography classes, tends the vegetable garden, and runs errands. Kabir's parents run a garment shop; his mother also volunteers teaching knitting to women in the community. His brother Rohan is a paid software engineer who also volunteers on weekends teaching computer skills.

"When you are doing any work, do not think of anything beyond. Do it as worship, as the highest worship, and devote your whole life to it for the time being." — Swami Vivekananda

2. What is their contribution to our everyday lives?
2 Economic activities

Economic activities involve money or money's worth: a business person selling school bags in a market, a farmer selling produce, a lawyer earning a fee, a truck driver, factory workers. Geeta Aunty's piloting is economic — she receives a salary, even though she also serves the country.

3 Non-economic activities

Non-economic activities don't generate income or wealth, but are done out of gratitude, love, care and respect: parents cooking for the family, helping with schoolwork, youth caring for grandparents, family helping renovate the house. Rohan's paid job is economic; his weekend volunteering is non-economic — the same person can do both.

4 Types of pay — Kavya's family

Kavya's uncle, a construction technician operating a bulldozer near a new highway (cutting travel time from 5 hours to 2), earns a monthly salary. Kavya's aunt works at the village post office (salary) and also teaches online exam-prep classes for a weekly fee. Sahil, a farm labourer, earns a daily wage — part in cash, part in mangoes of equal value, which is called payment in kind.

5 Value addition — Rajesh the carpenter

Rajesh (Kavya's father), a carpenter: buys wood for ₹600 → builds a chair using tools and skill → sells it for ₹1,000. The extra ₹400 is the monetary value of his skill, time and effort — this is value addition. Because payment is involved at every stage, these are economic activities.

Worksheet check Creates monetary value: Tailor, Doctor, Scientist. Does NOT: farmers repairing their own tractor, parents cooking dinner for their family, a person caring for a sick grandparent — no payment is exchanged.
6 The Importance of Non-Economic Activities — sevā

Non-economic activities generate real value even without money. Sevā (selfless service) is seen at temples, gurudwaras, mosques and churches — e.g. langars (community kitchens) serving free food. These foster satisfaction and gratitude, and contribute to society without expecting anything in return.

7 The strength of community participation

Swachh Bharat Abhiyan: the collective effort of Indian citizens to clean streets, roads, parks and public areas — leading to a clean home, neighbourhood, society and nation. Van Mahotsav ('festival of forests'): community tree-plantation drives to promote awareness of trees and forest conservation.

Before we move on…
  • In this chapter, we learnt about economic and non-economic activities.
  • We also learnt about the value addition that economic activities create.
  • We understood how non-economic activities contribute to social welfare, personal wellbeing and enhance the overall quality of life.

Important Rules & Formulas

  • Economic activity = involves money/money's worth. Non-economic activity = no payment, done out of love/care/gratitude.
  • Value addition = Selling price − Cost of raw material (e.g. Rajesh's chair: ₹1,000 − ₹600 = ₹400).
  • Pay types: salary (fixed, regular, monthly), fee (for professional services), wage (for a period of work), payment in kind (non-cash).
  • One person can do both economic and non-economic activities.

Quick Summary

  • Economic activities involve money/money's worth; non-economic activities don't, but still create real value.
  • Pay comes as salary, fee, wage, or payment in kind.
  • Value addition = the extra value created by skill, time and effort (Rajesh's chair: ₹600 wood → ₹1,000 chair = ₹400 added).
  • The same person can do both economic (paid job) and non-economic (volunteering) activities.
  • Sevā — langars, temple prasād — contributes to society without expecting anything back.
  • Swachh Bharat Abhiyan and Van Mahotsav show the strength of collective, non-economic community participation.
  • Non-economic activities boost social welfare, personal wellbeing, and quality of life.
