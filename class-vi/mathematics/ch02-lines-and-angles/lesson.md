## Overview
This chapter builds the vocabulary of geometry — point, line segment, line, ray and angle — then shows how to compare angles, measure them in **degrees** with a protractor, and classify them as acute, right, obtuse, straight or reflex.

---

## Key Terms & Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Point | An exact location. It has no length, breadth or thickness. Named with a capital letter: point P. |
| Line segment | The shortest path between two points; it has **two end points**. Written AB (same as BA). |
| Line | A line segment extended endlessly in **both** directions. No end points; cannot be measured. Written ↔AB or with a small letter, line *l*. |
| Ray | A part of a line that starts at one point and goes on endlessly in **one** direction. Written →OP (start point first). Ray OP ≠ ray PO. |
| Angle | Formed by **two rays with a common starting point**. |
| Vertex | The common starting point of the two rays of an angle. |
| Arms | The two rays that form an angle. |
| Degree (°) | Unit for measuring angles; a full turn = 360°. |
| Protractor | Instrument for measuring and drawing angles (0°–180°, with an inner and an outer scale). |
| Acute angle | Greater than 0° and less than 90°. |
| Right angle | Exactly 90° — a quarter turn. |
| Obtuse angle | Greater than 90° and less than 180°. |
| Straight angle | Exactly 180° — a half turn; the arms point in opposite directions. |
| Reflex angle | Greater than 180° and less than 360°. |
| Complete angle | 360° — a full turn. |
| Perpendicular lines | Two lines that meet at a right angle. |
| Interior of an angle | The region between the two arms. |

## Core Concepts

### 1. Point, line segment, line, ray
- Through **one** point, countless lines can pass.
- Through **two** points, **exactly one** line passes.
- A line segment can be measured (it has two end points); a line and a ray cannot (they go on forever).
- A ray is named starting from its start point: ray AB starts at A and passes through B.

### 2. Angle and how to name it
- An angle is named with three letters, **vertex in the middle**: ∠ABC or ∠CBA (vertex B). If only one angle is at a vertex, ∠B is enough.
- An angle can be seen as the **amount of turn** of one arm about the vertex from the other arm (door opening, scissors, clock hands).

### 3. Comparing angles
- By **superimposition**: place one angle over the other with the vertices and one arm matching; the one whose other arm is further out is larger.
- Arm **length does not matter** — only the opening (amount of turn).

### 4. Special angles
- Full turn = 360°; half turn = straight angle = 180°; quarter turn = right angle = 90°.
- Why 360? It has many factors (divisible by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12 …) so many fractions of a turn are whole numbers; it is also close to the number of days in a year.

### 5. Measuring an angle with a protractor
1. Place the protractor's centre point on the vertex.
2. Line up the base line (0°) with one arm.
3. Read where the other arm crosses the scale — use the scale that **starts at 0° on the arm you aligned** (inner or outer).
- **Common mistake:** reading the wrong scale gives 180° − correct answer (e.g. reading 50° instead of 130°). Check: is it acute or obtuse by eye?

### 6. Drawing an angle (e.g. 125°)
1. Draw a ray BC.
2. Place the protractor centre at B, base line along BC.
3. Starting from 0° on BC's side, mark a dot A at 125°.
4. Join BA. ∠ABC = 125°.
- To draw a **reflex** angle like 240°: draw 360° − 240° = 120° and mark the **outside** angle as 240°.

### 7. Angles on a clock and compass
- Clock: 12 hour marks in 360° → **30° per hour mark**. Minute hand turns 6° per minute; hour hand turns 0.5° per minute.
  - 3:00 → 90°, 4:00 → 120°, 6:00 → 180°, 9:00 → 90°.
  - 2:30 → minute hand at 180°, hour hand at 75° → 105°.
- Compass: N → E (clockwise) = 90°; N → S = 180°; N → W clockwise = 270°. Between neighbouring 8-point directions (N, NE, E, …) = 45°.

## Important Rules / Formulas
- Full turn = 360°, straight = 180°, right = 90°.
- Reflex angle = 360° − the smaller angle.
- Angle needed to complete a right angle = 90° − angle; to complete a straight angle = 180° − angle.
- Line segments formed by *n* points on a line = 1 + 2 + … + (*n* − 1) (a triangular number).
- Angles formed by *n* rays from one vertex (all within a straight angle) = 1 + 2 + … + (*n* − 1).

## Solved Examples
### Example 1
Five points A, B, C, D, E lie on a line in that order. How many line segments do they make?
**Solution:**
1. From A: AB, AC, AD, AE (4). From B: BC, BD, BE (3). From C: CD, CE (2). From D: DE (1).
2. Total = 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = **10**.

### Example 2
What is the smaller angle between the hands at 5:00? Classify it.
**Solution:** 5 hour marks × 30° = **150°** — obtuse. The reflex angle is 360° − 150° = 210°.

## Quick Summary
- Segment: 2 end points; ray: 1 start point; line: none.
- Angle = two rays + common vertex; name with vertex in the middle.
- Acute < 90° = right < obtuse < 180° = straight < reflex < 360° = complete.
- Protractor: centre on vertex, base on one arm, read the scale that starts at 0° on that arm.
