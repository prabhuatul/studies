## Overview
Mathematics is, at heart, the search for **patterns** and the explanation of **why** they happen. The chapter looks at famous number sequences, draws them as pictures to see why they behave as they do, finds surprising relations between sequences, and then finds the same number sequences hiding inside patterns of shapes.

## Key Terms & Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Pattern | A regularity that repeats or grows by a rule, in numbers or shapes. |
| Number sequence | A list of numbers that follows a rule, e.g. 2, 4, 6, 8, … |
| Counting (natural) numbers | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, … |
| Odd numbers | Numbers that leave remainder 1 when divided by 2: 1, 3, 5, 7, … |
| Even numbers | Multiples of 2: 2, 4, 6, 8, … |
| Triangular numbers | 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, … — dots that can be arranged in a triangle. *n*-th triangular number = 1 + 2 + … + *n*. |
| Square numbers | 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, … — dots that can be arranged in a square; *n* × *n*. |
| Cube numbers | 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, … — *n* × *n* × *n*. |
| Virahānka numbers | 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, … — each number is the **sum of the two before it**. Named after the Prakrit scholar Virahānka (c. 600–800 CE), who found them while counting poetic rhythms. Known in Europe later as Fibonacci numbers. |
| Powers of 2 | 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, … — each is double the previous. |
| Powers of 3 | 1, 3, 9, 27, 81, … — each is three times the previous. |
| Hexagonal numbers | 1, 7, 19, 37, 61, … — dots arranged in a hexagon. |
| Regular polygon | A closed figure with all sides equal and all angles equal. |
| Complete graph | A set of points with **every** pair of points joined by a line. |
| Koch snowflake | A shape made by repeatedly replacing the middle third of every side with a "tent" (two sides of a small equilateral triangle). |

## Core Concepts

### 1. What is mathematics?
Mathematics is the search for patterns and for explanations of why they exist. Patterns appear in nature, in art, in music, in calendars and clocks — and mathematics gives the tools to describe and predict them.

### 2. The ten basic number sequences (Table 1)
| Sequence | First terms | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| All 1's | 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 | constant |
| Counting numbers | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | add 1 |
| Odd numbers | 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 | add 2, start at 1 |
| Even numbers | 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 | add 2, start at 2 |
| Triangular numbers | 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 | add 2, then 3, then 4, … |
| Squares | 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 | *n* × *n* |
| Cubes | 1, 8, 27, 64, 125 | *n* × *n* × *n* |
| Virahānka numbers | 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 | sum of previous two |
| Powers of 2 | 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 | × 2 |
| Powers of 3 | 1, 3, 9, 27, 81 | × 3 |

### 3. Visualising sequences
Drawing a sequence as dots explains its name: triangular numbers stack into triangles (1, then a row of 2 underneath, then a row of 3 …), squares form *n* × *n* grids, cubes form *n* × *n* × *n* blocks, hexagonal numbers form hexagons.

### 4. Relations among number sequences (the key results)
- **Adding odd numbers gives squares:** 1 = 1, 1 + 3 = 4, 1 + 3 + 5 = 9, … The sum of the first *n* odd numbers = *n* × *n*.
  *Why:* each new odd number is an "L-shaped" layer that wraps around the previous square to make the next square.
  Example: 1 + 3 + 5 + … + 19 (the first 10 odd numbers) = 10 × 10 = **100**.
- **Adding up and down gives squares:** 1 + 2 + 1 = 4, 1 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 9, 1 + 2 + … + *n* + … + 2 + 1 = *n* × *n*.
- **Adding counting numbers gives triangular numbers:** 1, 1+2 = 3, 1+2+3 = 6, …
- **Adding two consecutive triangular numbers gives a square:** 1 + 3 = 4, 3 + 6 = 9, 6 + 10 = 16.
- **Adding all 1's gives counting numbers:** 1, 1+1 = 2, 1+1+1 = 3, …
- **Powers of 2:** 1 + 2 + 4 + … + (a power of 2) = (next power of 2) − 1. E.g. 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 15 = 16 − 1.
- **Powers of 3:** (1 + 3 + 9 + … ) × 2 + 1 = next power of 3. E.g. (1 + 3 + 9) × 2 + 1 = 27.
- **Hexagonal numbers:** triangular number × 6 + 1 = hexagonal number (1×6+1 = 7, 3×6+1 = 19, 6×6+1 = 37). Adding hexagonal numbers gives cubes: 1, 1 + 7 = 8, 1 + 7 + 19 = 27, 1 + 7 + 19 + 37 = 64.
- **Adding cubes gives squares of triangular numbers:** 1 + 8 = 9 = 3², 1 + 8 + 27 = 36 = 6².

### 5. Patterns in shapes (Table 3)
- **Regular polygons:** triangle (3 sides), quadrilateral/square (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6), heptagon (7), octagon (8), nonagon (9), decagon (10). Dodecagon = 12 sides.
- **Complete graphs:** join every pair of *n* points.
- **Stacked squares:** 1 × 1, 2 × 2, 3 × 3 … arrangements of small squares.
- **Stacked triangles:** a big triangle split into rows of small triangles.
- **Koch snowflake:** start with an equilateral triangle; on every side replace the middle third by two sides of a smaller triangle; repeat.

### 6. Relating shapes to numbers
| Shape pattern | Count | Sequence |
|---|---|---|
| Regular polygons | number of sides / corners: 3, 4, 5, 6 … | counting numbers from 3 |
| Complete graphs (2, 3, 4, 5, 6 points) | lines: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 | triangular numbers |
| Stacked squares | small squares: 1, 4, 9, 16 | square numbers |
| Stacked triangles (1, 2, 3, 4 rows) | small triangles: 1, 4, 9, 16 | square numbers |
| Koch snowflake | sides: 3, 12, 48, 192, 768 | 3 × powers of 4 |

**Why complete graphs give triangular numbers:** adding a new point means joining it to every old point. With 5 points the new point adds 4 lines, so lines = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10.

**Koch snowflake perimeter grows:** each step, every side becomes 4 sides that are each ⅓ as long, so the perimeter is multiplied by 4/3 each time. Example: side 27 cm → stage 0 perimeter 3 × 27 = 81 cm; stage 1 has 12 sides of 9 cm = 108 cm.

## Important Rules / Formulas
- **Sum of first *n* odd numbers** = *n* × *n*
- ***n*-th triangular number** = 1 + 2 + … + *n* = *n* × (*n* + 1) ÷ 2
- **Two consecutive triangular numbers add to a square.**
- **1 + 2 + 4 + … + 2ⁿ** = 2ⁿ⁺¹ − 1
- **Lines in a complete graph with *n* points** = (*n* − 1)-th triangular number = *n* × (*n* − 1) ÷ 2
- **Diagonals of a polygon with *n* sides** = lines of complete graph − sides (e.g. pentagon: 10 − 5 = 5; octagon: 28 − 8 = 20)
- **Virahānka rule:** next = sum of previous two.

## Solved Examples
### Example 1
Find 1 + 3 + 5 + … + 29 without adding.
**Solution:**
1. Count the odd numbers from 1 to 29: (29 + 1) ÷ 2 = 15.
2. Sum of first 15 odd numbers = 15 × 15 = **225**.

### Example 2
How many lines join every pair of 7 points?
**Solution:**
1. Lines = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 (each new point joins all previous points).
2. = **21** (the 6th triangular number).

### Example 3
Write the next three Virahānka numbers after 13, 21.
**Solution:** 13 + 21 = 34, 21 + 34 = 55, 34 + 55 = **89**.

## Figures & Diagrams
### Triangular numbers as dots
Rows of 1, 2, 3, 4 dots stacked into a triangle → 1, 3, 6, 10 dots.
### Odd numbers building squares
A 1 × 1 square, then an L of 3 dots around it gives 2 × 2, an L of 5 gives 3 × 3, and so on.
### Complete graphs
2 points joined by 1 line; 3 points → triangle (3 lines); 4 points → square with both diagonals (6 lines); 5 points → pentagon with a star inside (10 lines).

## Quick Summary
- 10 basic sequences: all 1's, counting, odd, even, triangular, square, cube, Virahānka, powers of 2, powers of 3.
- Sum of first *n* odd numbers = *n*².
- Counting numbers add to triangular numbers; consecutive triangular numbers add to squares.
- Complete graph lines = triangular numbers; stacked squares & stacked triangles = squares; Koch snowflake sides = 3 × 4, 3 × 4 × 4, …
