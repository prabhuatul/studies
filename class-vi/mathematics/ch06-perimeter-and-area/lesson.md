## Overview
**Perimeter** is the length of the boundary of a closed figure; **area** is the amount of region it covers. The chapter finds perimeters of rectangles, squares, triangles and regular polygons, finds areas by counting unit squares and by formula, gets the area of a triangle as half a rectangle, and shows that figures with the same perimeter can have different areas (and vice versa).

---

## Key Terms & Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Perimeter | The total length of the boundary of a closed figure. Unit: cm, m, km. |
| Area | The amount of region (surface) enclosed by a closed figure. Unit: square units — cm², m². |
| Unit square | A square of side 1 unit; area 1 square unit. |
| Regular polygon | All sides equal and all angles equal. |
| Tangram | A square cut into 7 pieces (5 triangles, 1 square, 1 parallelogram) used to make shapes. |

## Core Concepts

### 1. Perimeter
- Rectangle: *l* + *b* + *l* + *b* = **2 × (*l* + *b*)**
- Square: **4 × side**
- Triangle: **sum of the three sides**; equilateral: 3 × side
- Regular polygon with *n* sides: ***n* × side** (pentagon perimeter 100 cm → side 20 cm)
- Finding a missing side: rectangle perimeter 25 cm, breadth 5 cm → *l* + *b* = 12.5 → *l* = **7.5 cm**.
- **Rounds of a track:** distance = number of rounds × perimeter (Akshi and Toshi running problem).
- Cost of fencing = perimeter × rate per metre (subtract any gate left open).

### 2. Area
- By counting squares on squared paper: count full squares; count a square that is **more than half** covered as 1; ignore less than half; two half squares make 1.
- Rectangle: ***l* × *b***; Square: **side × side**.
- Number of tiles = area of floor ÷ area of one tile (use the same units!).

### 3. Area of a triangle
A diagonal splits a rectangle into **two equal right triangles**, so a right triangle with legs *l* and *b* has area **½ × *l* × *b***. A triangle drawn inside a rectangle with the same base and the third vertex on the opposite side also has half the rectangle's area.

### 4. Same perimeter, different areas (and the reverse)
- Rectangles with perimeter 24 cm (whole-number sides): 1×11 (11), 2×10 (20), 3×9 (27), 4×8 (32), 5×7 (35), 6×6 (36) cm². **The square has the largest area.**
- Same area 36 cm²: 1×36 (perimeter 74), 2×18 (40), 3×12 (30), 4×9 (26), 6×6 (24). The square has the **smallest** perimeter.
- Cutting a square notch out of a corner of a rectangle: **area decreases**, but the **perimeter stays the same** (the two inner edges replace the two removed edge pieces).

## Important Rules / Formulas
- Perimeter of rectangle = 2(*l* + *b*); of square = 4*a*; of regular *n*-gon = *n* × side.
- Area of rectangle = *l* × *b*; of square = *a* × *a*; of right triangle = ½ × base × height.
- 1 m = 100 cm; 1 m² = 10,000 cm²; 1 km = 1000 m.

## Solved Examples
### Example 1
A hall 12 m × 9 m is tiled with square tiles of side 60 cm. How many tiles?
**Solution:**
1. 12 m = 1200 cm → 1200 ÷ 60 = 20 tiles along the length.
2. 9 m = 900 cm → 900 ÷ 60 = 15 tiles along the breadth.
3. Tiles = 20 × 15 = **300**.

### Example 2
Akshi runs 4 rounds of a 60 m × 40 m track; Toshi runs 5 rounds of a square track of side 35 m. Who runs more?
**Solution:** Akshi: 2 × (60 + 40) × 4 = 800 m. Toshi: 4 × 35 × 5 = 700 m. **Akshi, by 100 m.**

---

## Quick Summary
- Perimeter = boundary length (cm); area = region covered (cm²).
- Rectangle: P = 2(*l* + *b*), A = *l* × *b*. Square: P = 4*a*, A = *a*².
- Right triangle = half a rectangle.
- Same perimeter → square has max area; same area → square has min perimeter.
