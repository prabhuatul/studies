Verbs, Helping Verbs, Modals and Tenses

Verbs and their forms, helping verbs, subject–verb agreement, modals, the nine tenses, changing tenses, and the textbook's grammar exercises.

Key Terms & Definitions

14 Every term you need to know
TermDefinition
VerbA word that tells us about an action (run, write) or a state (is, have, like).
Main verbThe verb that carries the main meaning in a sentence — e.g. serving in "The mahout was serving food."
Helping (auxiliary) verbA verb that helps the main verb show tense, questions or negatives — forms of be, do, have.
V1 / V2 / V3Base form / past form / past participle — e.g. write, wrote, written.
Regular verbA verb whose V2 and V3 end in -d or -ed (look → looked → looked).
Irregular verbA verb with special V2/V3 forms (give → gave → given).
Subject–verb agreementA singular subject takes a singular verb (She is), a plural subject a plural verb (They are).
Modal verbA special helping verb that shows ability, permission, advice, necessity, possibility etc. — can, may, must, should…
TenseThe form of the verb that shows the time of an action — present, past or future.
Simple tenseShows a fact, habit or completed action (She writes / wrote / will write).
Continuous (progressive) tenseShows an action going on: be + V-ing (She is / was / will be writing).
Perfect tenseShows a completed action: have/has/had/will have + V3 (She has / had / will have written).
ConjunctionA joining word — and, but, or, because, so, although, unless.
Neither…nor / Either…orNeither…nor joins two negatives; either…or offers a choice between two things.

Spelling rules for -edEnds in e → add -d (serve → served). Consonant + y → -ied (try → tried). Short vowel + one consonant → double it (stop → stopped). Vowel + y → just -ed (play → played).
2 Helping (auxiliary) verbs — be, do, have
Helping verbFormsUsed forExample
beam, is, are, was, were, be, been, beingContinuous tensesThe birds are flying.
havehas, have, hadPerfect tensesBuntee had followed the farmer.
dodo, does, didQuestions, negatives, emphasisDid Gajaraj eat? He did not eat.

Main or helping? In "The mahout was serving food", serving is the main verb and was is helping. But in "The mahout was kind", was is the main verb (there is no other verb). The same is true for have: "I have a pen" (main) vs "I have finished" (helping).

With do/does/did, use V1"Did he go?" — never "Did he went?" "She does not like" — never "does not likes".

4 Modals — what each one does

Modals are special helping verbs. Rules: a modal is followed by the base form (V1); it never takes -s, -ing or -ed ("She can swim", not "can swims"). Need to, used to and ought to take "to".

The textbook's three past tenses (p.48)Simple past — action happened earlier (V2). Past progressive — action was going on (was/were + V-ing). Past perfect — action was completed (had + V3), usually before another past action.

7 Changing a sentence from one tense to another
  1. Find the subject and decide if it is singular or plural.
  2. Find the main verb and write its V1, V2, V3 and V-ing.
  3. Use the formula for the new tense (be + V-ing, have/has/had + V3, will + V1…).
  4. Make the helping verb agree with the subject. Keep the rest of the sentence the same (drop/change time words if needed).

8 Neither…nor, either…or (from The Unlikely Best Friends)

"…neither the farmer nor the mahout noticed that the two friends were in tears." — neither…nor joins two negative choices: neither of them noticed.

Either…or gives a choice between two things: "Rohit wants to play either cricket or kho-kho."

Important Rules & Facts

  • Modal + V1 (can go, must eat). With do/does/did, use V1 (did not go).
  • Continuous = be + V-ing; perfect = have/has/had + V3; future = will + V1.
  • He/She/It + is / was / has / V1+s; We/You/They + are / were / have / V1; I + am / was / have.
  • Past perfect shows the earlier of two past actions.
  • Never use present perfect with a finished past time word (yesterday, ago, last…).
  • many/few + countable; much/little + uncountable.

Solved Examples

1 Choosing the right past tense (Poorvi p.49)

"The farmer came back and ___ (noticed / was noticing / had noticed) that Buntee was sad. He ___ (tied / was tying / had tied) Buntee with a rope the day before. He ___ (asked / was asking / had asked) Buntee why he was sad. The farmer ___ (felt / was feeling / had felt) that Buntee ___ (missed / was missing / had missed) Gajaraj and set him free."

Solution: noticed (simple past, one finished action) · had tied (happened before another past action — "the day before") · asked · felt · was missing (a feeling going on at that time).

2 Modals in a dialogue (Poorvi p.97)

Ajay: Anand had fever last week. He (i) ___ take good rest. · Suman: Yes, he (ii) ___ or he will fall ill again. · Ajay: He (iii) ___ take grandmother's natural cures. · Suman: Yes, we (iv) ___ remember how effective they are.

Solution (one good set): (i) should (advice) · (ii) must (compulsion — or he will fall ill) · (iii) can / may (ability / suggestion) · (iv) need to (necessity). Each modal used once.

3 Tense change — worked

"The teacher explains the lesson." → past continuous.

  1. Subject: the teacher (singular).
  2. Verb: explain → explained → explained → explaining.
  3. Past continuous = was/were + V-ing.
  4. Singular → was: "The teacher was explaining the lesson."

Quick Summary

  • Verbs have three forms: V1, V2, V3. Learn the irregular ones.
  • Helping verbs be, do, have help the main verb form tenses, questions and negatives.
  • The verb must agree with its subject (is/are, was/were, has/have).
  • Modals show ability, permission, advice, compulsion, necessity, possibility or past habit, and are followed by V1.
  • Nine tenses: simple, continuous and perfect in present, past and future.
  • Time words signal the tense; change tense by changing only the verb group.
  • Neither…nor joins two negatives; either…or gives a choice.
  • Many/few for countable nouns; much/little for uncountable nouns.
