A · Fill in the blanks 10 marks

  1. The V3 form of "write" is ______________.
  2. Continuous tenses use a form of ______________ + V-ing.
  3. Perfect tenses use a form of ______________ + V3.
  4. The modal ______________ shows a past habit.
  5. The modal ______________ shows compulsion.
  6. "She ______________ (have/has) completed her work."
  7. "The birds ______________ (fly/are flying) in the sky now."
  8. "By the time we reached, the train ______________ (left/had left)."
  9. "There are ______________ (much/many) flowers in the garden."
  10. "I planted a sapling ______________ I wanted to help the environment." (because/but)
Answers
  1. written
  2. be
  3. have
  4. used to
  5. must
  6. has
  7. are flying
  8. had left
  9. many
  10. because

B · True or false 10 marks

  1. A modal is followed by the base form of the verb.
  2. "Did he went home?" is correct.
  3. "has", "have" and "had" are used to form perfect tenses.
  4. "Yesterday I have finished my homework" is correct.
  5. "should" is used to give advice.
  6. "Children" takes "is" in "The children ___ playing".
  7. Past perfect shows the earlier of two past actions.
  8. "much" is used with countable nouns.
  9. "was" is a helping verb in "The mahout was kind".
  10. "Neither the farmer nor the mahout noticed" means both did not notice.
Answers with reasons
  1. True — e.g. "She can sing", never "can sings".
  2. False — after did, use V1: "Did he go home?"
  3. True — perfect = have/has/had + V3.
  4. False — a finished past time (yesterday) needs simple past: "Yesterday I finished my homework."
  5. True — "You should try to remember these cures."
  6. False — children is plural, so it takes "are".
  7. True — e.g. "He had tied Buntee the day before" — earlier than setting him free.
  8. False — much goes with uncountable nouns (much water); many goes with countable (many flowers).
  9. False — here "was" is the main verb — there is no other verb.
  10. True — neither…nor joins two negatives.

D · Short answers 5 × 2 = 10 marks

D1 Underline the main verb and circle the helping verb: (a) The children are planting trees. (b) Gajaraj had eaten his food. (c) Does Amber like neem?

(a) main: planting, helping: are. (b) main: eaten, helping: had. (c) main: like, helping: Does.

D2 Change into the tense given: (a) Riya visits her grandmother every Sunday. (past) (b) The children play football. (present continuous)

(a) Riya visited her grandmother every Sunday. (b) The children are playing football.

D3 Change into the tense given: (a) My father drives me to school. (simple future) (b) The teacher explains the lesson. (past continuous)

(a) My father will drive me to school. (b) The teacher was explaining the lesson.

D4 Fill in suitable modals: (a) You ___ wear a seat belt; it is the law. (b) I ___ swim when I was six. (c) It ___ rain this evening; take an umbrella.

(a) must (compulsion). (b) could (past ability). (c) may/might (possibility).

D5 Correct the errors: (a) She can sings well. (b) They was playing. (c) He has wrote a letter.

(a) She can sing well. (b) They were playing. (c) He has written a letter.

E · Long answers 2 × 5 = 10 marks

E1 Write the sentence "Mario sits on the chair" in all nine tenses.

E2 Complete the passage with the correct form of the verbs in brackets: "Yesterday, Amber (come) home from school. While she (sit) under the neem tree, she (hear) a whisper. She (never talk) to a tree before. Today she (tell) her friends about it, and tomorrow she (write) in her diary."

Yesterday, Amber came home from school. While she was sitting under the neem tree, she heard a whisper. She had never talked to a tree before. Today she is telling (or tells) her friends about it, and tomorrow she will write in her diary.

Reasons: yesterday → simple past; "while…" → past continuous; before another past action → past perfect; today → present; tomorrow → future.

F · HOTS 2 × 3 = 6 marks

Higher-order thinking. Reason it out rather than recalling a fact.

F1 Explain the difference in meaning: (a) "I used to play the flute." (b) "I am used to playing the flute."

(a) used to + V1 is a modal of past habit — I played the flute regularly in the past, but I don't now. (b) be used to + V-ing means being familiar with something — playing the flute is normal and easy for me now. Same words, different grammar, different meaning.

F2 Why does the textbook use "had tied" but "asked" in the same passage about Buntee?

"He had tied Buntee with a rope the day before" happened earlier than the other past actions (coming back, noticing, asking), so it takes the past perfect. "He asked Buntee why he was sad" is part of the main sequence of events, so it is simple past. Past perfect shows which past action came first.

G · Textbook exercises — worked answers

The chapter's own "Let us enhance our learning" questions. Try each one first, then open it.

G1 Poorvi p.48 — sort the verbs into simple past, past progressive and past perfect.

Simple past: looked, gave, hated, noticed, went, served, told, asked, felt, touched, joined, arrived, threw.
Past progressive: was looking, was missing, was munching.
Past perfect: had ordered, had followed, had remained, had tied.

G2 Poorvi p.49 — select the correct form of the verb.

noticed · had tied · asked · felt · was missing.

G3 Poorvi p.82 — match present and past forms.

become–became · find–found · make–made · discover–discovered · tell–told · give–gave.

G4 Poorvi p.97 — match modals with their functions.

may – suggestion · should – advice · can – ability · must – compulsion · need to – necessity · used to – past habit.

G5 Poorvi p.97 — complete the dialogue with four modals (each once).

(i) should · (ii) must · (iii) can (or may) · (iv) need to. Other sensible sets are fine if each modal fits its function.
