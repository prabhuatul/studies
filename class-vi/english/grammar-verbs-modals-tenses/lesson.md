## Overview

This pack revises the **grammar for PT-2**: **verbs** and their three forms, **helping (auxiliary) verbs** — be, do, have — and **subject–verb agreement**; **modals** (can, could, may, might, must, should, would, will, shall, need to, used to, ought to) and the job each one does; and all nine **tenses** (simple, continuous and perfect in the present, past and future), with the **time words** that signal each one and a step-by-step method to **change a sentence from one tense to another**. It also covers the textbook's own grammar exercises from _The Unlikely Best Friends_, _Neem Baba_ and _Spices that Heal Us_, plus conjunctions and much/many/few/little, which appear on the school worksheet.

## Key Terms & Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Verb | A word that tells us about an action (run, write) or a state (is, have, like). |
| Main verb | The verb that carries the main meaning in a sentence — e.g. _serving_ in "The mahout was serving food." |
| Helping (auxiliary) verb | A verb that helps the main verb show tense, questions or negatives — forms of **be**, **do**, **have**. |
| V1 / V2 / V3 | Base form / past form / past participle — e.g. write, wrote, written. |
| Regular verb | A verb whose V2 and V3 end in -d or -ed (look → looked → looked). |
| Irregular verb | A verb with special V2/V3 forms (give → gave → given). |
| Subject–verb agreement | A singular subject takes a singular verb (She is), a plural subject a plural verb (They are). |
| Modal verb | A special helping verb that shows ability, permission, advice, necessity, possibility etc. — can, may, must, should… |
| Tense | The form of the verb that shows the time of an action — present, past or future. |
| Simple tense | Shows a fact, habit or completed action (She writes / wrote / will write). |
| Continuous (progressive) tense | Shows an action going on: be + V-ing (She is / was / will be writing). |
| Perfect tense | Shows a completed action: have/has/had/will have + V3 (She has / had / will have written). |
| Conjunction | A joining word — and, but, or, because, so, although, unless. |
| Neither…nor / Either…or | Neither…nor joins two negatives; either…or offers a choice between two things. |

## Core Concepts

### Verbs and their three forms

Verbs tell us what someone **does** or **is**. Every verb has three main forms:

| V1 (base) | V2 (past) | V3 (past participle) | V1 (base) | V2 | V3 |

| look | looked | looked | give | gave | given |

| serve | served | served | go | went | gone |

| notice | noticed | noticed | tell | told | told |

| discover | discovered | discovered | become | became | become |

| plant | planted | planted | find | found | found |

| visit | visited | visited | make | made | made |

| complete | completed | completed | write | wrote | written |

| try | tried | tried | eat | ate | eaten |

| stop | stopped | stopped | fly | flew | flown |

| play | played | played | see | saw | seen |

| help | helped | helped | take | took | taken |

| explain | explained | explained | throw | threw | thrown |

| — |  |  | drive | drove | driven |

| — |  |  | feel | felt | felt |

| — |  |  | bring / buy / think | brought / bought / thought | brought / bought / thought |

| — |  |  | put / cut / read | put / cut / read | put / cut / read |

> **Spelling rules for -ed:** Ends in **e** → add -d (serve → served). Consonant + **y** → -ied (try → tried). Short vowel + one consonant → double it (stop → stopped). Vowel + y → just -ed (play → played).

### Helping (auxiliary) verbs — be, do, have

| Helping verb | Forms | Used for | Example |

| **be** | am, is, are, was, were, be, been, being | Continuous tenses | The birds are flying. |

| **have** | has, have, had | Perfect tenses | Buntee had followed the farmer. |

| **do** | do, does, did | Questions, negatives, emphasis | Did Gajaraj eat? He did not eat. |

**Main or helping?** In "The mahout was serving food", _serving_ is the main verb and _was_ is helping. But in "The mahout was kind", _was_ is the main verb (there is no other verb). The same is true for have: "I have a pen" (main) vs "I have finished" (helping).

> **With do/does/did, use V1:** "Did he **go**?" — never "Did he went?" "She does not **like**" — never "does not likes".

### Subject–verb agreement

| Subject | be (present) | be (past) | have | Simple present |

| I | am | was | have | I play |

| He / She / It / a singular noun | is | was | has | She plays (add -s/-es) |

| We / You / They / a plural noun | are | were | have | They play |

- "My best friend **is** always helpful." (singular) · "My friends **are** helpful." (plural)

- With **neither…nor / either…or**, the verb agrees with the nearer subject: "Neither Shikha nor Anuj **likes** to tell stories."

- -es after s, sh, ch, x, o: watch → watches, go → goes; consonant + y → -ies: fly → flies.

### Modals — what each one does

Modals are special helping verbs. **Rules:** a modal is followed by the **base form (V1)**; it never takes -s, -ing or -ed ("She can **swim**", not "can swims"). _Need to_, _used to_ and _ought to_ take "to".

| Modal | Function | Example |

| **can** | ability; informal permission | It can help control cough and cold. · Can I go out? |

| **could** | past ability; polite request | I could swim at six. · Could you help me? |

| **may** | formal permission; possibility; suggestion | You may share them with your friends. |

| **might** | weak possibility | It might rain later. |

| **must** | compulsion; strong necessity | You must consult an elder before you use them. |

| **should** | advice; duty | You should try to remember these cures. · We should respect our elders. |

| **ought to** | moral duty | We ought to help the needy. |

| **need to** | necessity | I need to soak the methi seeds overnight. |

| **used to** | past habit | I used to put heeng water on your tummy. |

| **will / shall** | future; promise (shall with I/we, formal) | I will try my best. · Shall we go? |

| **would** | polite request; past habit | Would you like some tea? |

The first six example sentences are from Daadi's letter in _Spices that Heal Us_ (p.96–97).

### The nine tenses — formulas

|  | Simple | Continuous | Perfect |

| **Present** | V1 / V1+s
_She writes._ | am/is/are + V-ing
_She is writing._ | has/have + V3
_She has written._ |

| **Past** | V2
_She wrote._ | was/were + V-ing
_She was writing._ | had + V3
_She had written._ |

| **Future** | will/shall + V1
_She will write._ | will be + V-ing
_She will be writing._ | will have + V3
_She will have written._ |

> **The textbook's three past tenses (p.48):** **Simple past** — action happened earlier (V2). **Past progressive** — action was going on (was/were + V-ing). **Past perfect** — action was completed (had + V3), usually before another past action.

### When to use each tense — time words

| Tense | Use | Signal words |

| Simple present | habits, facts, routines | always, usually, often, every day/Sunday, never |

| Present continuous | happening right now | now, at present, at the moment, Look!, Listen! |

| Present perfect | just finished; result matters now | already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for |

| Simple past | finished at a past time | yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020 |

| Past continuous | going on at a past time | while, when, at 5 pm yesterday, all evening |

| Past perfect | finished before another past action | before, after, by the time, already (in past) |

| Simple future | will happen later | tomorrow, next week, soon, in future |

| Future continuous | will be going on later | at this time tomorrow |

| Future perfect | will be finished by a future time | by tomorrow, by next year |

> **Don't mix:** "Yesterday" never goes with present perfect: "I **finished** it yesterday", not "I have finished it yesterday".

### Changing a sentence from one tense to another

- Find the **subject** and decide if it is singular or plural.

- Find the **main verb** and write its V1, V2, V3 and V-ing.

- Use the formula for the **new tense** (be + V-ing, have/has/had + V3, will + V1…).

- Make the helping verb **agree** with the subject. Keep the rest of the sentence the same (drop/change time words if needed).

| Sentence | Change to | Answer |

| Amber sits under the neem tree. | Simple past | Amber **sat** under the neem tree. |

| The dog eats the food. | Present continuous | The dog **is eating** the food. |

| Daadi writes a letter. | Simple future | Daadi **will write** a letter. |

| The children were singing. | Simple present | The children **sing**. |

| Mario tries the chair. | Past continuous | Mario **was trying** the chair. |

| We plant saplings. | Present perfect | We **have planted** saplings. |

| She finishes her homework. | Present perfect | She **has finished** her homework. |

| The bird flew beyond the tree. | Past perfect | The bird **had flown** beyond the tree. |

### Neither…nor, either…or (from The Unlikely Best Friends)

"…**neither** the farmer **nor** the mahout noticed that the two friends were in tears." — _neither…nor_ joins **two negative** choices: neither of them noticed.

**Either…or** gives a choice between two things: "Rohit wants to play **either** cricket **or** kho-kho."

- Neither Shikha nor Anuj likes to tell stories.
- Either Bhavesh or Abha made this painting.

### Also on the worksheet — conjunctions and much/many

| Conjunction | Job | Example |

| and | adds | Gajaraj and Buntee played together. |

| but | contrast | The mahout was kind, but he was not a friend. |

| or | choice | Would you like tea or milk? |

| because | reason | I planted a sapling because I wanted to help the environment. |

| so | result | It rained, so we stayed in. |

| although | unexpected contrast | Although he was weak, Buntee ran to the stable. |

| unless | "if not" | You will not pass unless you practise. |

**Many / few** — with things you can **count** (many flowers, few friends). **Much / little / less** — with things you **cannot count** (much water, little time).

## Important Rules / Formulas

- Modal + **V1** (can go, must eat). With do/does/did, use **V1** (did not go).
- Continuous = **be + V-ing**; perfect = **have/has/had + V3**; future = **will + V1**.
- He/She/It + **is / was / has / V1+s**; We/You/They + **are / were / have / V1**; I + **am / was / have**.
- Past perfect shows the **earlier** of two past actions.
- Never use present perfect with a finished past time word (yesterday, ago, last…).
- **many/few** + countable; **much/little** + uncountable.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Choosing the right past tense (Poorvi p.49)

"The farmer came back and ___ (noticed / was noticing / had noticed) that Buntee was sad. He ___ (tied / was tying / had tied) Buntee with a rope the day before. He ___ (asked / was asking / had asked) Buntee why he was sad. The farmer ___ (felt / was feeling / had felt) that Buntee ___ (missed / was missing / had missed) Gajaraj and set him free."

**Solution:** noticed (simple past, one finished action) · had tied (happened _before_ another past action — "the day before") · asked · felt · was missing (a feeling going on at that time).

### Example 2: Modals in a dialogue (Poorvi p.97)

Ajay: Anand had fever last week. He (i) ___ take good rest. · Suman: Yes, he (ii) ___ or he will fall ill again. · Ajay: He (iii) ___ take grandmother's natural cures. · Suman: Yes, we (iv) ___ remember how effective they are.

**Solution (one good set):** (i) **should** (advice) · (ii) **must** (compulsion — or he will fall ill) · (iii) **can / may** (ability / suggestion) · (iv) **need to** (necessity). Each modal used once.

### Example 3: Tense change — worked

**"The teacher explains the lesson."** → past continuous.

- Subject: the teacher (singular).
- Verb: explain → explained → explained → explaining.
- Past continuous = was/were + V-ing.
- Singular → **was**: "The teacher **was explaining** the lesson."

## Quick Summary

- Verbs have three forms: V1, V2, V3. Learn the irregular ones.
- Helping verbs **be, do, have** help the main verb form tenses, questions and negatives.
- The verb must agree with its subject (is/are, was/were, has/have).
- Modals show ability, permission, advice, compulsion, necessity, possibility or past habit, and are followed by V1.
- Nine tenses: simple, continuous and perfect in present, past and future.
- Time words signal the tense; change tense by changing only the verb group.
- Neither…nor joins two negatives; either…or gives a choice.
- Many/few for countable nouns; much/little for uncountable nouns.
